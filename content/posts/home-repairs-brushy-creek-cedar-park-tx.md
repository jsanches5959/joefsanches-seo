# Home Repairs in Brushy Creek, Cedar Park TX: The Long-Span List for 1985-and-Newer Creek and Trail Homes

Brushy Creek is one of Central Texas's most unusual communities - a master-planned development governed by its own Municipal Utility District, the Brushy Creek MUD, which provides water, wastewater, parks and recreation services directly to residents. The result is a park system on a scale no neighborhood HOA could match: over 3,000 acres of parks, trails, sports complexes, swimming pools and recreation centers, anchored by the Brushy Creek Trail, a 30-plus-mile multi-use trail that connects through Cedar Park and Round Rock. Homes run about $320,000 to $480,000, there is no separate HOA on top of the MUD, and the community profile lists Leander ISD - Cypress Elementary, Cedar Park Middle School and Cedar Park High School - while the southern sections near Anderson Mill feed other districts, so exact campus assignment is worth verifying by address before buying.

That build span is the point. Brushy Creek homes went up from 1985 through 2012, so the oldest sections are 40 years in, second-generation systems are aging alongside original ones in the later sections, and creek and trail lots carry moisture and drainage work that an interior subdivision never sees. This is the Brushy Creek list we see, what each item costs in the area, and the order to tackle it in.

## 1. Exterior paint and caulk across four decades of sections

On a 1985 to 2012 Brushy Creek home, no two streets are on the same paint clock. The 1980s and 1990s sections have been repainted several times and the most recent coat is usually at the end of its run in Central Texas sun; the 2000s sections are due or overdue for their second full cycle. The south and west elevations fail first, caulk joints open at the trim and around windows, and the brick, stucco and stone elevations common here need crack repair and the right masonry primer before paint, not paint over cracks. The order matters more than the brand: repair first, then caulk, prime and paint. Our [Cedar Park painting page](/services/interior-exterior-painting-cedar-park-tx) covers what a full repaint includes, and our [exterior paint guide](/posts/exterior-paint-texas-heat-how-long-lasts) covers how long a good job lasts in this heat. One Brushy Creek advantage: with the MUD handling the neighborhood-wide services and no separate HOA layer, exterior upkeep decisions sit with the homeowner - confirm any current MUD or section rules before the crew, not after.

## 2. Settling cracks in the oldest ground in the set

Brushy Creek ground has the longest wet-dry history of any Cedar Park community in this series - sections graded in the 1980s have seen four decades of cycles over limestone and clay. Inside, that shows up as the classic pattern: a diagonal crack over a doorway, a ceiling seam that reappears in the same joint, nail pops along one wall. At this age the movement is almost always cosmetic settling, not foundation failure - our [drywall cracks guide](/posts/drywall-cracks-when-to-worry-central-texas) walks through which cracks are which. The cracks that earn a specialist's look first are the ones that are wide, offset from one side to the other, growing fast, or paired with doors that suddenly stick.

The repair that lasts is cut back to solid material, re-tape, float, and match the existing texture before paint. Brushy Creek interiors carry layers of textures and repaints from different decades, which is less forgiving: a patch that skips the texture and sheen match shows every time the afternoon sun comes across the wall. Details are on the [Cedar Park drywall page](/services/drywall-repair-cedar-park-tx).

## 4. Creek lots, mature trees, decks and drainage

Brushy Creek was built along the creek it is named for, and its mature canopy and greenbelt lots are the trade for a real moisture workload. The oaks run on Central Texas rules:

- **Timing.** Oak wilt spreads through fresh cuts during the high-risk months. The safe window runs July through January - which means right now, late fall and winter, is when Brushy Creek oak work should happen. Our [live oak trimming guide](/posts/live-oak-trimming-central-texas) covers raising, thinning and reducing, and what never to do to an oak.
- **Removal is the last resort, priced by size.** Removal in the Austin area averages about $736, with most jobs between $256 and $1,962 depending on height and access, and a large tree can need a permit first - we flag which applies before quoting. The [Cedar Park tree page](/services/tree-removal-cedar-park-tx) has the full breakdown, and our [tree removal permit guide](/posts/tree-removal-permit-leander-cedar-park-austin) covers how Cedar Park handles it.

The creek and trail lots carry the rest of this section. Decks and patios in greenbelt shade hold moisture, so boards and joist ends rot earlier than the same structure on an open lot - repairs run $852-$3,345, and a rebuild runs $30-$50 per square foot, most projects $3,946-$10,909, with composite the common pick on shaded lots because it costs more up front and almost nothing to maintain. See the [Cedar Park deck and patio page](/services/deck-patio-builder-cedar-park-tx) for how outdoor builds are scoped. Drainage gets a hard look on every creek-side walk-through - water should move away from the slab toward the street and the creek corridors, not pond against the house after a heavy rain, and low lots need that checked before any deck or fence money goes in. Wood fences on the older sections have been replaced at least once by now; the current generation is on the repair clock - pickets cupping and graying, gates sagging as posts move in clay. A sound fence can be repaired section by section; failed posts are the rebuild trigger.

## What order to do it in

1. **Water first.** Caulk, open stucco and stone cracks, drainage running toward the house - anything letting water in outranks everything cosmetic, and creek lots get this look first.
2. **Trees in the safe window.** Oak work between July and January, while the window is open - the mature canopy does not get cheaper or smaller for waiting.
3. **Systems check.** Original and second-generation systems get a licensed look before they fail in August, not after.
4. **Surfaces.** Paint, then the finish refresh if the older package is going, then washing as the finishing pass.

## Frequently asked questions

**What breaks first in a 1985 to 2012 Brushy Creek home?**
It depends on the section, and that is the honest answer for this community. In the oldest sections it is second-generation systems, the current paint cycle, and fence and deck wood; in the 2000s sections it is original systems reaching replacement age and the first full repaint. Everywhere, the mature oaks run on the oak-wilt calendar and creek lots carry drainage work the interior streets do not. The structure itself, on a maintained home, is rarely the problem.

**How much does it cost to repaint a Brushy Creek house?**
Interior rooms run $350 to $650 for a bedroom, walls only, and a whole 2,000 sq ft interior runs $4,000 to $8,000 for walls or $6,500 to $12,000 with ceilings and trim. Exterior pricing depends on size, stories, elevation materials and how much caulk and crack repair the prep uncovers, so it is quoted as a written estimate after a walk-through rather than a per-foot guess.

**Are the drywall cracks in my Brushy Creek home a foundation problem?**
Usually not. Lots over limestone and clay move slightly with every wet and dry cycle, and after two to four decades the diagonal cracks over doorways and reappearing ceiling seams are the normal signature of that movement. Cracks that are wide, growing fast, offset from one side to the other, or paired with doors suddenly sticking deserve a foundation specialist's look before any cosmetic repair.

**When should I trim my Brushy Creek live oaks?**
Between July and January. Oak wilt spreads through fresh pruning cuts most easily in the spring high-risk period, so late fall and winter are the safe working window in Central Texas. Trimming runs about $245 to $1,029 depending on size and canopy, and cuts should be painted immediately as part of the job. Brushy Creek canopies are mature, so the scope is usually real canopy work, not a street-tree trim.

**Does Brushy Creek have an HOA I need approval from for repairs?**
Most of Brushy Creek does not have a traditional HOA - the Brushy Creek MUD serves the neighborhood-wide functions HOAs perform elsewhere, funded through the MUD tax rate rather than monthly dues. For like-for-like repairs that is generally a simpler path. Anything that changes the exterior footprint, or removing a large tree, can still need a city or county permit, and exact school assignment varies by section and address, so confirm both for your specific address. We flag which applies during the walk-through and get any needed approval in hand before work starts.
