# Tree Removal in Cedar Park, TX: Local Guide to Costs, Rules and Common Trees

Cedar Park sits right on the edge of the Hill Country, and its trees show it: big live oaks in established neighborhoods, cedar on the western slopes, and plenty of hackberries and cedar elms along fence lines. Here's what Cedar Park homeowners should know before removing or trimming a tree.

## The trees we see most in Cedar Park

- **Live oaks:** the signature shade trees in older neighborhoods. They need clearance trimming over roofs and driveways, and must follow oak wilt timing. See [live oak trimming](/posts/live-oak-trimming-central-texas).
- **Ashe juniper (cedar):** common on the west side. Often removed for cedar fever, fire safety and to give oaks room. See [cedar tree removal](/posts/cedar-tree-removal-ashe-juniper-leander).
- **Cedar elms:** tough native shade trees, usually worth keeping and trimming.
- **Hackberries:** fence-line volunteers with weak wood that often should go. See [hackberry removal](/posts/hackberry-tree-removal-texas).
- **Arizona ash and other fast growers** planted in the 1990s and 2000s, many now declining.

## Rules to check first

- **Your HOA.** Many Cedar Park neighborhoods require architectural approval before tree removal, especially front-yard trees. See [HOA tree removal rules](/posts/hoa-tree-removal-rules-leander-tx).
- **The city.** Cedar Park's tree rules apply mostly to development and construction, but check with the city for very large trees, trees near the street or right-of-way, or work tied to a building permit.
- **Austin city limits.** Some addresses near Cedar Park are actually in the City of Austin, which has strict protected-tree rules. See [tree removal permits](/posts/tree-removal-permit-leander-cedar-park-austin).

## Oak wilt in Cedar Park

Oak wilt has been a recurring problem across the Austin area. Avoid pruning or wounding oaks from **February through June**, and paint every cut. Live oaks in neighborhoods often share root systems, so one infected tree can affect a whole block. See [when to trim oak trees](/posts/when-to-trim-oak-trees-central-texas).

## Common Cedar Park tree problems

- **Limbs over the roof** in established neighborhoods with mature oaks.
- **Drought-stressed trees** after hot, dry summers. See [signs a tree is dying](/posts/signs-tree-is-dying-central-texas).
- **Fence-line trees** damaging fences and leaning into neighbors' yards. See [neighbor's tree overhanging your yard](/posts/neighbor-tree-branches-overhanging-texas-law).
- **Storm damage** from spring wind and occasional ice.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need a permit to remove a tree in Cedar Park?**
For a typical backyard tree on an existing home, usually not from the city, but check your HOA. Contact the city for very large trees, trees near the street, or work tied to construction. Some nearby addresses are in Austin, which has stricter rules.

**How much does tree removal cost in Cedar Park?**
Roughly $250 to $700 for small trees, $600 to $1,800 for medium, and $1,500 to $4,000 or more for large trees and big live oaks, depending on access and what's underneath.

**When should I trim oak trees in Cedar Park?**
July through January. Avoid February through June because of oak wilt, and paint every cut.
