# Tree Removal & Cedar Clearing in Liberty Hill, TX: A Local Guide

Liberty Hill is a mix of brand-new subdivisions and Hill Country acreage. That means two very different kinds of tree work: trimming and removing yard trees on newer lots, and clearing cedar and brush across bigger properties. Here's what to know about both.

## Subdivision lots: protect the trees you have

Many newer Liberty Hill neighborhoods were built on land that was cedar, scrub and scattered oaks. The oaks that were saved during construction often struggle years later because their roots were compacted or cut. If you're in a newer subdivision:

- **Watch your saved oaks** for thinning canopies and dead limbs. See [signs a tree is dying](/posts/signs-tree-is-dying-central-texas).
- **Water deeply during drought** and keep mulch over the root zone.
- **Check your HOA** before removing anything. See [HOA tree removal rules](/posts/hoa-tree-removal-rules-leander-tx).

## Acreage: cedar and brush clearing

On larger Liberty Hill properties, the most common job is clearing cedar (Ashe juniper) and brush:

- **around the house and outbuildings** for fire safety (see [wildfire defensible space](/posts/wildfire-defensible-space-trees-hill-country)),
- **under oak mottes** to give live oaks room and water,
- **along fence lines and drives** for access,
- **to open up views and pasture.**

Selective clearing that keeps the best oaks and some cedar for screening and erosion control is usually the best result. See [lot and brush clearing costs](/posts/lot-clearing-cost-leander-tx) and [cedar removal](/posts/cedar-tree-removal-ashe-juniper-leander).

## Hill Country terrain matters

Liberty Hill's shallow soil, limestone and slopes affect tree work:

- **Rock limits stump grinding depth** in many spots.
- **Slopes and ledges** slow equipment and affect pricing.
- **Shallow-rooted trees** on thin soil can be more prone to tipping in storms.

## Oak wilt

Live oak mottes are common on Liberty Hill acreage, and connected roots mean oak wilt can spread through a whole motte. Avoid wounding oaks February through June, and paint every cut. See [when to trim oak trees](/posts/when-to-trim-oak-trees-central-texas).

## Habitat considerations on larger tracts

In parts of the Hill Country, mature Ashe juniper woodland can be habitat for the endangered golden-cheeked warbler. If you own a larger tract with mature cedar woodland and plan large-scale clearing, check first.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much does it cost to clear cedar in Liberty Hill?**
Roughly $1,000 to $3,000 per acre for light brush, and $2,500 to $6,000 or more for dense cedar, depending on terrain, rock and how selective the clearing is.

**Should I clear all the cedar on my property?**
Usually not. Clearing near the house and under oaks, and thinning thick stands, while keeping some mature cedar, is often the best balance.

**When is the best time for tree work in Liberty Hill?**
Oak work July through January. Cedar can be cleared any time, though many people clear before winter pollen season.
