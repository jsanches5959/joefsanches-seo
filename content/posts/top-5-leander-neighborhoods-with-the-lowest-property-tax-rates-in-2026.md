# Top 5 Leander-Area Neighborhoods with the Lowest Property Tax Rates in 2026

Two houses at the same price, a mile apart, can carry property tax bills that differ by more than $1,500 a year. In the Leander area the biggest reason is usually a MUD (municipal utility district): newer master-planned communities pay for their roads, water and sewer lines through an extra tax that older neighborhoods don't have.

So the lowest-tax neighborhoods around Leander share a pattern. They're older, they were built before MUD financing became the norm, and most of them sit on the Cedar Park side of the line while still zoned to Leander ISD.

The rates below are approximate combined rates (city or county, Leander ISD, Austin Community College, Williamson County, and any special districts) from the neighborhood guides on this site. Always confirm a specific address on its tax bill before you make an offer. Rates change every year, and a single street can sit in a different district than its neighbors.

## 1. Buttercup Creek

Built mostly between the early 1990s and 2008, Buttercup Creek is one of the most established neighborhoods in Cedar Park and one of the lowest-tax addresses zoned to Leander ISD. There's no MUD on top of the base rates, and the HOA is light. The trade-off is age: expect original systems, roofs and finishes in many homes, so budget for updates and get a thorough inspection.

## 2. Forest Oaks

Forest Oaks is a smaller, quieter Cedar Park neighborhood from the late 1990s and early 2000s, with mature trees and one of the lowest HOAs on this list. Like Buttercup Creek, its low rate comes from having no MUD. Homes here tend to be priced in the mid-$300s, which makes it one of the better values for buyers who want Leander ISD without a new-construction tax rate.

## 3. Block House Creek

Block House Creek straddles the Cedar Park and Leander area and dates mostly from the 1990s to the mid-2000s. It's consistently among the lowest combined rates in the area, with established infrastructure and a modest HOA. Check the individual address: the neighborhood is large, and district boundaries are exactly where tax surprises come from.

## 4. Twin Creeks

Twin Creeks is newer (roughly 2004–2018) and more upscale than the first three, with a country club and golf course, and it still carries a lower rate than most new construction in Leander. The HOA is higher than the older neighborhoods on this list, so add both numbers together when you compare it against a MUD community with a lower HOA.

## 5. Crystal Falls

Crystal Falls is the only neighborhood on this list inside the City of Leander, and its combined rate is still well below the newer master-planned communities around it. It's hilly, with views, trails and a golf course, and homes range from the 2000s to new builds. Some newer sections carry different assessments than the original phases, so confirm the rate on the specific home.

## How to check the real tax bill for a home

1. Look up the property on the Williamson Central Appraisal District site (or Travis CAD for homes south of the county line) and note every taxing unit listed.
2. Add the current rates for each unit. The MUD line, if there is one, is usually the biggest variable.
3. Ask for the seller's most recent tax bill. Remember that their exemptions (homestead, over-65, disabled veteran) don't transfer to you.
4. File your own homestead exemption as soon as you close. It's the single biggest reduction most owners qualify for.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why are older neighborhoods cheaper to own in Leander?**
Most were built before developers used MUDs to finance infrastructure. Newer communities repay that debt through an extra tax rate, often for 20 years or more.

**Is a lower tax rate always the better deal?**
Not always. A neighborhood with a higher rate but newer construction can have lower repair costs, and HOA dues can close part of the gap. Compare the total monthly cost of owning: mortgage, taxes, HOA, insurance and expected maintenance.

**Do Leander property tax rates change every year?**
Yes. Every taxing unit sets its rate annually, and appraised values change too. Treat any rate older than the current tax year as an estimate.

**Can I protest my appraisal if my taxes look too high?**
Yes. In Williamson County you can protest your appraised value with the appraisal district each spring after notices go out.
