# Should You Remove That Hackberry Tree? A Central Texas Homeowner's Guide

If there's a tree growing out of your fence line that you never planted, there's a good chance it's a hackberry. Birds eat the berries and drop the seeds along fences, and hackberries grow fast. Here's how to decide whether yours should stay.

## How to recognize a hackberry

- **Bark** that's gray with distinctive corky ridges or warts.
- **Leaves** that are lopsided at the base, with toothed edges and a rough, sandpapery texture on some types.
- **Small berries** that turn orange-red to dark purple in fall.
- **Location:** often right against a fence, the house foundation or under another tree.

## Why people remove hackberries

- **Weak wood.** Hackberries grow quickly and their wood is relatively weak, so they tend to drop limbs, especially as they get older.
- **Bad locations.** Because birds plant them, they often grow tight against fences, foundations, AC units and driveways, where trunks and roots cause damage.
- **Shorter lifespan and decay.** They're prone to decay and are often declining by the time they're large.
- **Mess.** Berries, sticky residue from insects on the leaves in some years, and lots of seedlings.

## When to keep one

A healthy hackberry in a good spot, away from the house with room to grow, can be a perfectly fine shade tree. They tolerate drought and poor soil, and wildlife like the berries. If it's well-placed and solid, regular deadwood removal may be all it needs.

## Remove it while it's small

The best time to remove a fence-line hackberry is when it's young. A thin sapling is a quick job. Wait ten years and the same tree has a big trunk pushing on the fence and limbs over two yards.

## Do hackberries grow back?

Yes. Hackberries can resprout from the stump if it's just cut. Grinding the stump, or treating fresh cut stumps of small saplings, helps prevent regrowth. See [stump grinding costs](/posts/stump-grinding-cost-leander-cedar-park).

## What hackberry removal costs

Rough planning ranges, not quotes:

- **Small fence-line hackberry:** often $150 – $400
- **Medium tree (20–40 ft):** often $400 – $1,200
- **Large hackberry over a structure:** more, depending on access and what's underneath

If it's growing through a fence, budget for a fence repair after the trunk comes out.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are hackberry trees good or bad?**
They're tough and good for wildlife, but have weak wood, decay easily and often grow in bad spots. Well-placed healthy ones can stay; fence-line volunteers usually should go.

**Do hackberry trees drop branches?**
Yes, more than many trees, especially as they age or when decay sets in.

**Will a hackberry grow back after I cut it down?**
It can resprout from the stump. Grinding the stump helps prevent it.

**How do I stop hackberries growing along my fence?**
Pull seedlings when they're small, and remove saplings before the trunks get large.
