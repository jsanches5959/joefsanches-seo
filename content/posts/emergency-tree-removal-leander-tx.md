# Emergency Tree Removal in Leander & Cedar Park: What to Do After a Storm

Central Texas weather is hard on trees. Spring thunderstorms bring straight-line winds, summer drought weakens limbs, and the rare ice storm, like February 2023, can bring down more trees in a night than a normal year. Here's how to handle tree damage when it happens.

## Is it really an emergency?

**Call right away if:**

- a tree or limb is on your house, garage or car,
- a tree is leaning badly toward the house and the soil around the roots is heaved or cracked,
- a downed tree is blocking your driveway or the only way in and out,
- a broken limb is hanging in the canopy over a walkway or play area (often called a "hanger").

**It can usually wait a few days if:**

- the tree or limb is down in an open part of the yard,
- it's across a fence with nothing else at risk,
- it's a split or cracked limb that isn't over anything important. Keep people and pets away from it until then.

**If power lines are involved,** it's the utility's job first. Stay away and call your electric provider. See [trees near power lines](/posts/tree-near-power-lines-texas).

## What to do while you wait

1. Keep everyone away from the damaged tree and anything under it.
2. Photograph everything for insurance before it's moved. See [tree fell on my house: who pays?](/posts/tree-fell-on-house-texas-insurance)
3. If water is getting in, put buckets and towels down and move belongings. Don't climb onto the roof.
4. Don't run a chainsaw on a tree that's under tension or resting on something. Storm-damaged wood can spring or roll without warning.

## What emergency tree removal costs

Emergency work typically runs about **1.5 to 2 times** normal rates, because it's done fast, often in tough conditions, and usually means cutting a tree off a structure in controlled pieces. After a big storm, demand is high across the whole area, so the most urgent jobs (trees on houses) get done first.

## Watch out for storm chasers

After big storms, out-of-area crews go door to door. Some are fine. Some aren't. Protect yourself:

- **Get a written quote** before work starts, listing what's included (removal, haul-off, stump).
- **Don't pay the full amount upfront.**
- **Get a local phone number and address.**
- **Be wary of anyone who pressures you** to sign today or to let them handle your insurance claim.

## After the emergency: check your other trees

A storm that brings down one tree often cracks limbs on others. Look for:

- hanging or broken branches in the canopy,
- new cracks in the trunk or where big limbs join,
- a tree that has started leaning since the storm,
- lifted soil on one side of the trunk.

A quick check and some cleanup now is much cheaper than the next emergency.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much does emergency tree removal cost?**
Typically 1.5 to 2 times normal removal rates, depending on how dangerous and complicated the job is. A tree on a roof is the most expensive scenario.

**Will insurance pay for emergency tree removal?**
If the tree damaged a covered structure, many policies help with removal from the structure. Trees that fell in open yard usually aren't covered.

**Can I cut up a fallen tree myself?**
A tree lying flat in open yard, maybe, if you have experience. Never cut a tree that's resting on a structure, hung up in another tree, or touching lines.

**How quickly can you come out after a storm?**
It depends on the storm. Trees on houses and blocked driveways are prioritized. Use the form and mark it urgent, or call.
