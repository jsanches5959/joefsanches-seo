# Water-Damaged Drywall: Patch, Replace, or Worry About Mold?

A brown ring has appeared on your ceiling. Or the wall behind the shower feels soft. Or there is a stain above the baseboard that was not there last month.

Before anything else: **the drywall is the symptom.** Painting over it is putting makeup on a warning light.

Here is how to work out what you are dealing with, in the order that saves money.

---

## Step one: find the water

Do not skip this. Every dollar spent on drywall before the source is fixed is a dollar spent twice.

### Where it usually comes from

**Ceiling stain, upstairs bathroom above** — shower pan, wax ring at the toilet, or a supply line. The most common cause of a first-floor ceiling stain in a two-storey house.

**Ceiling stain, roof above** — roof penetration, flashing at a chimney or vent, or ice-dam style backup at a valley. Stains often appear away from the actual entry point because water travels along framing before it drops.

**Wall stain near a window** — failed exterior caulk or flashing. Common in Central Texas after a driving rain from an unusual direction.

**Wall stain at the base** — a supply or drain line in the wall, a dishwasher or washing machine, or exterior grading pushing water at the slab.

**Wall stain with no obvious source** — an HVAC condensate line. Extremely common here and frequently overlooked. The drain pan overflows or the line clogs with algae, and water appears somewhere seemingly unrelated. If your stain appeared in summer with the AC running hard, check this first.

### Confirm before you repair

- Dry the area fully, mark the stain edge with a pencil and date it. If it grows, the leak is active.
- A cheap moisture meter tells you more than looking does. Compare a suspect area with known-dry wall a few feet away.
- If it is plumbing, the plumber comes first. If it is roofing, the roofer comes first.

**We will not patch over an active leak**, and neither should anyone else.

## Step two: can it be dried, or does it come out?

Drywall is gypsum in paper. Paper is the vulnerable part — it is organic, and wet organic material grows things.

### Usually can be dried and saved

- **Surface staining only**, substrate still firm
- Wet for **under about 48 hours**
- **Clean water** — supply line, rainwater, condensate
- No softness when you press it
- Paper face intact, not delaminating

Dry it aggressively with air movement and dehumidification, verify with a meter, then stain-block and repaint. A regular primer will not hold back a water stain — it needs a **stain-blocking primer**, usually shellac or oil-based, or the tannin bleeds straight through your new paint within weeks.

### Must be replaced

- **Soft, crumbling, or you can push a finger into it**
- **Sagging ceiling** — this is also a safety matter; wet drywall overhead is heavy and can come down
- **Paper separating** from the gypsum core
- Wet for **more than about 72 hours**
- Visible **mold growth**
- **Category 2 or 3 water** — dishwasher or washing machine discharge, and anything involving sewage

Replacement is not as dramatic as it sounds. The standard approach is a **flood cut** — cut the wall horizontally a foot or two above the highest water line, remove everything below, dry the cavity and framing properly, then rebuild. It gives access to dry the structure, which is the part that actually matters.

**Also check the insulation.** Wet fiberglass loses its R-value and holds water against the framing. Wet insulation comes out, always.

## The mold question, proportionately

Mold needs moisture, an organic food source and time. Drywall paper is an excellent food source. The commonly cited window is around **24 to 48 hours** of sustained wetness before growth starts.

**What is normal:** a small area of surface mold on drywall from a one-off leak that has been fixed. Clean it, dry it, or cut out the affected section. Most homeowners can handle a patch smaller than about ten square feet.

**What warrants a professional:** the EPA guidance commonly cited is that areas larger than roughly **10 square feet** are worth professional remediation. Also: mold inside wall cavities, contamination in HVAC, sewage-related water, or anyone in the house with asthma, respiratory conditions or immune compromise.

**What is not proportionate:** panic. A stain is not mold. Mold on a small patch of drywall from a fixed leak is a normal repair.

**Do not** dry-sand or aggressively brush mold — it aerosolises spores. Contain the area, keep it damp while removing, bag the debris.

**Black mold specifically:** *Stachybotrys* gets a lot of press. It needs sustained saturation over time, so it is associated with long-running unfixed leaks rather than a weekend's water. Colour alone does not identify a species, and for remediation purposes the species mostly does not change what you do: find the water, remove the material, dry the structure.

## Insurance, briefly

Policies differ, so read yours — but the general pattern in Texas:

**Usually covered:** sudden and accidental discharge. A pipe bursts, a water heater fails, a supply line lets go. Typically covers the resulting damage.

**Usually not covered:** long-term seepage and gradual damage. The leak that has been dripping for eight months is treated as a maintenance failure. Also generally excluded: flood (needs separate flood insurance), and in many policies, the cost of fixing the failed component itself as opposed to the damage it caused.

**Mold coverage is often capped** at a modest sub-limit, or excluded unless it results from a covered event.

**If you may claim:**
- Photograph everything before touching anything
- Keep receipts, including for emergency mitigation
- Report promptly — delay is a common reason for denial
- Do take reasonable steps to stop further damage; policies require it

Weigh a small claim against your deductible and the effect on your premium.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can water-damaged drywall be saved?**
Sometimes. If it was wet less than about 48 hours with clean water, is still firm, and the paper face is intact, dry it thoroughly and stain-block it. If it is soft, sagging, delaminating, or was wet longer, it needs to come out.

**How long does it take for mold to grow on wet drywall?**
Commonly cited as 24 to 48 hours under the right conditions. This is why drying speed matters more than almost anything else after a leak.

**Do I need to replace drywall after a small leak?**
Not necessarily. A small stain from a fixed leak, on drywall that is still firm and dry, usually needs a stain-blocking primer and paint. Press the area — if it gives at all, it comes out.

**Why does the water stain keep coming back through my paint?**
Water stains are tannins that bleed through ordinary latex paint. You need a stain-blocking primer, typically shellac-based or oil-based, before repainting. Two coats of wall paint over a bare stain will fail every time.

**What causes a water stain on the ceiling with no plumbing above?**
In Central Texas, most often the HVAC condensate drain — a clogged line or overflowing pan in the attic. Roof leaks are the other common cause, and they frequently show up well away from the actual entry point because water runs along framing before dropping.

**Is a sagging ceiling dangerous?**
Yes. Saturated drywall is heavy and can fall in a sheet. Keep people out from underneath, and do not poke at it casually — if it is holding water, it will come down all at once. This is one to get looked at promptly.

**Should I run a dehumidifier or just open windows?**
A dehumidifier with air movers, almost always. Central Texas outdoor air is often more humid than indoor air, so opening windows can add moisture rather than remove it. Closed room, dehumidifier, fans moving air across wet surfaces.

## The summary

The stain is information. Read it before you cover it.

Find the water, fix the water, then decide whether the drywall dries or comes out — firm and briefly wet can be saved, soft or long-wet cannot. Mold on a small patch after a fixed leak is a normal repair, not an emergency; a large area or a cavity full of it is a job for a remediation contractor.

And never, ever paint over a stain without a stain-blocking primer and a dry substrate behind it.
