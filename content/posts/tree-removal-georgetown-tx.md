# Tree Removal in Georgetown, TX: Heritage Trees, Costs and What to Know

Georgetown has some of the most impressive old trees in Williamson County: big live oaks and pecans in the older neighborhoods near downtown and along the San Gabriel River, plus a mix of cedar, cedar elm and hackberry in newer subdivisions. Georgetown also takes its large trees seriously, so it's worth knowing the rules before you cut.

## Check Georgetown's heritage tree rules first

Georgetown protects large "heritage" trees. If you're inside city limits and planning to remove a big tree, especially a large oak, pecan or cedar elm, **contact the city before scheduling the work**. Requirements can depend on the tree's size and species and whether the work is tied to development.

Also check:

- **Your HOA**, including big communities like Sun City, which has its own rules. See [HOA tree removal rules](/posts/hoa-tree-removal-rules-leander-tx).
- **Whether you're in city limits or the county.** Some Georgetown addresses are outside the city.

More on how permits work across the area: [tree removal permits](/posts/tree-removal-permit-leander-cedar-park-austin).

## Trees common in Georgetown

- **Live oaks and Texas red oaks:** follow oak wilt timing. See [when to trim oak trees](/posts/when-to-trim-oak-trees-central-texas).
- **Pecans:** large, valuable shade trees, often in older neighborhoods. They can drop big limbs and need deadwood removal.
- **Cedar elms:** tough natives, generally worth keeping.
- **Ashe juniper (cedar):** common on undeveloped land and western lots.
- **Hackberries:** common fence-line volunteers. See [hackberry removal](/posts/hackberry-tree-removal-texas).

## Oak wilt in Georgetown

Avoid pruning or wounding oaks February 1 through June 30, and paint every cut. Live oaks frequently share root systems, so a single infection can spread through a neighborhood.

## Keep it if you can

Big, healthy heritage trees add real value and shade to a Georgetown property. Often the right answer is targeted trimming: deadwood removal, roof clearance and some thinning, not removal. If a tree is dead, decaying or dangerous, removal is the right call, and the city process is usually more straightforward for hazardous trees with documentation.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need a permit to remove a tree in Georgetown, TX?**
Possibly. Georgetown protects large heritage trees. Contact the city before removing a large tree inside city limits, and check your HOA.

**How much does tree removal cost in Georgetown?**
Roughly $250 to $700 for small trees, $600 to $1,800 for medium, and $1,500 to $4,000 or more for large oaks and pecans, depending on access and location.

**Can I trim a heritage tree?**
Routine, proper pruning is generally fine, but heavy pruning of protected trees may have rules. Check with the city if you're planning major work.
