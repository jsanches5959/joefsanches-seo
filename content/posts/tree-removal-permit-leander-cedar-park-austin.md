# Do I Need a Permit to Remove a Tree in Leander, Cedar Park or Austin?

Removing a tree without the right approval can mean fines, a required replacement tree, or a letter from your HOA. The rules depend on which city you're in, the size of the tree, and whether you're in an HOA.

This is a plain-English guide, not legal advice. Rules change, so always confirm with your city and HOA before cutting.

## The short version

| Where | What to check |
|---|---|
| City of Austin | Permit required for protected (19 in.+ diameter) and heritage trees |
| Leander | City tree rules apply mainly to development and some protected trees; check before removing a very large tree |
| Cedar Park | Tree preservation rules apply mainly to development; check before removing a very large tree |
| Georgetown | Has protections for large "heritage" trees; check with the city first |
| Any HOA | Most require approval for removal, especially in front yards |

## Austin: the strictest in the area

Austin's tree ordinance is one of the toughest in Texas.

- **Protected trees:** generally trees **19 inches or larger in diameter**, measured 4.5 feet above the ground, need a permit to remove.
- **Heritage trees:** certain species (including many oaks, pecans and cedar elms) **24 inches or larger in diameter** have even stronger protection and a tougher approval process.
- **Dead or dangerous trees** still usually need the city's sign-off. Approval is typically easier, but you should document the condition.

If you're in Austin city limits, including parts of northwest Austin near Cedar Park, assume a big tree needs a permit until you've confirmed otherwise.

## Leander and Cedar Park

Leander and Cedar Park focus their tree rules mostly on **new development and construction**: subdivisions, commercial sites and permitted building work. For an existing home, removing a typical backyard tree generally doesn't need a city permit. Rules can still apply to very large trees, trees in easements or right-of-way, and work tied to a building permit.

Because the specifics change and depend on your property, call the city's planning or development services office if:

- the tree is very large (roughly 24 in.+ across),
- it's near the street, sidewalk or a drainage easement, or
- you're removing it as part of a remodel, addition or pool.

## Georgetown

Georgetown is known for protecting its large heritage trees, which are common in its older neighborhoods and around the San Gabriel River. If you're removing a big tree inside Georgetown city limits, check with the city before scheduling the work.

## Don't skip your HOA

In Leander and Cedar Park, the HOA is often the bigger hurdle. Many require an architectural review request before you remove any tree, especially in a front yard, and some require you to replace it. More detail in [HOA tree removal rules](/posts/hoa-tree-removal-rules-leander-tx).

## Trees on a property line or in an easement

- **Shared trees:** a tree whose trunk sits on the property line belongs to both owners. Get your neighbor's agreement in writing before removing it.
- **Utility easements:** trees in a utility easement may be managed by the utility. Never work near power lines yourself. See [trees near power lines](/posts/tree-near-power-lines-texas).

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need a permit to cut down a tree in my backyard in Leander?**
For a typical backyard tree on an existing home, usually not from the city. Check with your HOA, and contact the city if the tree is very large, near the street or tied to construction.

**What size tree is protected in Austin?**
Generally trees 19 inches in diameter or larger, measured 4.5 feet above ground. Heritage trees of certain species at 24 inches or more have stronger protection.

**Can I remove a dead tree without a permit in Austin?**
Dead and hazardous trees still usually need city review, though approval is typically more straightforward. Take photos documenting the tree's condition.

**What happens if I remove a protected tree without a permit?**
Cities can issue fines and require replacement trees. HOAs can also fine you or require replanting.
