# VA Approved Builders in Leander TX: What Changed in 2025 (and What It Means for Your Build)

Search "VA approved builders Leander" and you will find pages telling you to look for a builder's VA Builder ID number — and to walk away from any builder who does not have one. That advice is out of date. **Effective March 31, 2025, the VA eliminated the Builder ID requirement for standard VA-guaranteed loans on new and proposed construction (VA Circular 26-25-1).** There is no VA roster of approved builders for a regular VA purchase or construction loan anymore, and a builder who shows you an old ID number is showing you a credential the VA no longer issues for this purpose.

Here is what "VA approved" actually means in 2026, what did not change, and how a veteran in Leander, Cedar Park or Georgetown gets a home built, bought or remodeled without tripping over rules that no longer exist — or missing the ones that still do.

## The short answer

- **Before March 31, 2025:** a builder on a VA new-construction loan needed a VA-issued Builder ID.
- **Today:** for a standard VA-guaranteed loan, the VA no longer registers builders. **Your lender approves the builder** — license and insurance where Texas requires them, a complete set of plans and specs, a clean contract, and a draw schedule the lender can administer.
- **Still VA-registered:** builders on **Specially Adapted Housing (SAH) grants** and **Native American Direct Loans (NADL)** — the Builder ID still applies to those two programs only.
- **Unchanged:** the finished home still has to meet the VA's Minimum Property Requirements, a VA appraisal still decides value, and new construction still carries a builder warranty (one year, or a ten-year insured protection plan).
- **Buying an existing home with a VA loan:** there is no builder to approve at all. What matters is the appraisal and the repair list that comes with it.

## What your lender actually checks now

With the VA out of the builder-registration business for standard loans, the lender's file review is the real screen. Lenders in the Leander market consistently ask a builder for the same core package:

1. **Licensing and insurance.** Texas has no state general-contractor license, so the check lands on the trades: electrical, plumbing and HVAC work must be performed by contractors licensed through the state (TDLR and the plumbing board). General liability and workers' comp binders are part of the package.
2. **Complete plans and specifications.** The VA appraiser values the home from the plans. Incomplete drawings stall the appraisal, and the appraisal stalls everything behind it.
3. **A clean construction contract.** The contract price sets the loan amount, so lenders read it closely — scope, allowances and change-order terms included.
4. **A workable draw schedule.** Construction funds release in stages as work is verified. A builder who has never run a draw schedule learns it on your timeline.
5. **Fluency in VA property standards.** The home has to pass the VA's standards at final inspection. Building to them from day one is cheaper than fixing them at the end.

None of that requires a government ID number. All of it is verifiable in one conversation, before you sign anything — ask for the insurance binder, the partner license numbers that will appear on your contract, and a sample draw schedule.

## Remodels, adaptations and the SAH exception

A remodel on a home you already own is not a VA construction loan, and no VA builder approval applies to it. The one exception worth knowing by name is the **Specially Adapted Housing grant**: when the VA funds accessibility construction for a service-connected disabled veteran — roll-in showers, widened doors, ramps, accessible kitchens — the builder **does** still register with the VA and hold a Builder ID for that work. If you are planning an SAH-funded project, ask about registration in the first conversation; it is a short process, but it has to be in place before the work is set up, not discovered after.

For non-SAH accessibility work — the same roll-in shower or ramp paid conventionally — the rules are the ordinary Texas ones: licensed partners on the regulated trades, permits where the city requires them, and a contract that says exactly what is being built.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is there a list of VA approved builders in Leander TX?**
Not anymore for standard loans. The VA eliminated the Builder ID requirement for VA-guaranteed loans on new and proposed construction effective March 31, 2025 (Circular 26-25-1). Today the builder is approved by your VA lender, file by file. Anyone pointing you to an official VA builder list for a regular purchase or construction loan is working from pre-2025 information.

**Does my builder still need a VA Builder ID for anything?**
Yes — two programs kept it: Specially Adapted Housing (SAH) grants and the Native American Direct Loan (NADL) program. Builders on those projects still register with the VA. For everything else, no ID is issued or required.

**What does a lender look for in a builder on a VA construction loan?**
Licensing and insurance (in Texas, that means licensed partners on the electrical, plumbing and HVAC trades, since the state has no general-contractor license), complete plans and specifications for the VA appraisal, a clean construction contract, and a draw schedule the lender can administer. The finished home must meet VA Minimum Property Requirements and carry a builder warranty.

**I'm buying an existing Leander home with a VA loan. Do I need an approved builder?**
No. There is no builder in that transaction. What you may need is a contractor for the VA appraisal repair list — the Minimum Property Requirement items the appraiser flags before closing. Those repairs run on the contract's timeline, so speed and photo documentation matter more than any credential.

**Can Sanches Group do SAH grant work?**
SAH-funded construction is the exception where VA builder registration still applies, and we walk through that registration as part of setting the project up — it has to be in place before the work is scheduled. The accessibility work itself — roll-in showers, ramps, widened doorways, accessible kitchens and baths — is core remodeling work for us, whether it is grant-funded or conventional.

**Does Texas license general contractors or home builders?**
No. Texas has no state license for general contractors or homebuilders, which is exactly why the lender's review and your own checks carry the weight now. The regulated trades are licensed: electrical and HVAC through TDLR, plumbing through the state plumbing board. On our projects those trades are performed by licensed partners, and their license numbers go on the quote and contract.
