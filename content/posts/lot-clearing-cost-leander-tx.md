# Lot & Brush Clearing Cost in Leander and Liberty Hill (2026)

West of I-35, a lot of land is covered in cedar, mesquite, vines and scrub. Whether you're getting a lot ready to build, opening up an overgrown backyard, or clearing a fence line on acreage, here's how clearing is priced.

## Ballpark ranges

Rough planning numbers, not quotes. Clearing varies more than almost any other tree work:

| Job | Typical range |
|---|---|
| Overgrown backyard or fence line | $500 – $2,500 |
| Light brush, per acre | $1,000 – $3,000 |
| Dense cedar and brush, per acre | $2,500 – $6,000+ |
| Selective clearing (keep good trees) | Usually more than clearing everything |

Cleared material can be hauled off, mulched on site or piled. Haul-off is often the biggest variable.

## What drives the price

- **Density.** A few scattered cedars is a different job from a wall of cedar and greenbrier.
- **Tree size.** Brush and saplings go fast. Big cedar and mesquite trunks take more time and equipment.
- **Terrain and rock.** Slopes, creek banks and limestone ledges, all common in the Leander and Liberty Hill area, slow equipment down.
- **Selective vs. full clearing.** Keeping the oaks and good trees while removing everything around them takes careful work and costs more per acre, but it's usually worth it.
- **What happens to the material.** Hauling it all away costs more than mulching it in place.
- **Access.** Can equipment get in, and is there room to work?

## Clearing for a build

If you're building:

- **Check with the city or county** about permits and tree protection before clearing, especially if the lot is inside city limits. See [tree removal permits](/posts/tree-removal-permit-leander-cedar-park-austin).
- **Mark the trees you want to keep** before anyone starts. Protect their root zones during construction. Construction damage is one of the most common reasons trees die years later.
- **Coordinate with your builder** on the footprint, driveway and septic or utility locations.

## Clearing on acreage

On larger rural tracts, consider:

- **Endangered species habitat.** In parts of the Hill Country, mature Ashe juniper woodland can be nesting habitat for the golden-cheeked warbler. Large-scale clearing of mature woodland can carry restrictions, and spring nesting season matters. Check before clearing large tracts.
- **Erosion.** Clearing slopes completely can lead to erosion. Leaving some cover helps.
- **Agricultural or wildlife valuation.** If your property has an ag or wildlife tax valuation, ask how clearing fits your management plan.

## Keep the oaks

Clearing cedar and brush from under live oaks can really improve the oaks. Follow oak wilt timing for any work that wounds oak limbs or roots (avoid February through June) and paint any oak wounds. See [when to trim oak trees](/posts/when-to-trim-oak-trees-central-texas) and [cedar removal](/posts/cedar-tree-removal-ashe-juniper-leander).

## Frequently asked questions

**How much does it cost to clear an acre of land in Leander?**
Roughly $1,000 to $3,000 for light brush and $2,500 to $6,000 or more for dense cedar and brush, depending on terrain, rock, selectivity and haul-off.

**Is it cheaper to mulch cleared brush on site?**
Usually, yes. Mulching in place avoids hauling and dump costs, and the mulch helps hold soil.

**Do I need a permit to clear my lot?**
It depends on your city or county and whether you're building. Check before clearing, especially inside city limits.

**Can you clear brush but keep my oak trees?**
Yes. Selective clearing is common and usually the best choice for the property, though it costs more than clearing everything.
