# What Chandelier Installation Actually Costs in Leander & Cedar Park (2026 Numbers)

Search "chandelier installation cost" and you get the price of the chandelier. That is the part you chose in a showroom. The installation is a different job: a fixture that can weigh as much as a person, hanging from an electrical box in your ceiling, wired and leveled at a height somebody decided — and in a two-storey Leander entry, that height decision happens eighteen feet in the air.

That is why one neighbour paid a few hundred dollars to swap a dining room fixture and the next one paid four figures for what sounds like the same job. The fixture was not the difference. The ceiling, the box and the wiring were.

Here is what chandelier installation actually costs in Leander, Cedar Park, Georgetown and greater Austin, and what moves the number.

## Why the range is so wide

### Swap vs. new location

The cheapest chandelier job is a like-for-like swap: old fixture down, new fixture up, same box, same switch, standard ceiling. That is the $100-to-$350 labour job, and it is most dining rooms in Leander and Cedar Park.

A chandelier going where no fixture has ever been is a different project — new box, new wire run, a switch leg, drywall opened and patched. That is the work inside the $220-to-$3,400 installed range, and most of the spread is how far the new location sits from power and whether the ceiling is open from an attic above or closed.

### The box is the safety question

A chandelier does not hang from the drywall. It hangs from the electrical box, and the box has to be rated for the fixture's weight. A standard box is fine for a light fixture; a heavy chandelier needs a fan-rated box or a brace tied into the joist, which adds roughly $100 to $200 when the existing box is not up to it. Fixtures over about 50 pounds always need that check. This is the line item to ask about by name — a quote that never mentions the box on a heavy fixture is a quote that skipped the part that keeps it in the ceiling.

### Ceiling height is the multiplier

At eight or nine feet, one electrician on a step ladder does the job. In the two-storey entries that are standard in Travisso, Crystal Falls, Bryson and the newer Cedar Park builds, the same fixture needs scaffolding or a tall ladder setup, a second pair of hands, and the fixture assembled and leveled in the air. HomeGuide puts high-ceiling replacement labour at $200 to $400 against $100 to $150 at standard height, and a new high-ceiling installation at $700 to $2,000 in labour. Height, more than the fixture, is what turns a $500 job into a $1,500 one.

Our work page shows a twenty-light chandelier we ran in a two-storey entry — at that height it is a rigging job as much as an electrical one: rated box, drop measured for the room rather than the ceiling, fixture lifted and leveled without marking the walls.

### Assembly is real labour

A chandelier rarely arrives as one piece. Crystal and multi-arm fixtures can take longer to assemble — arms, chain, crystals, bulbs — than to hang. A fixture with a hundred crystals is priced like the hours it takes, because it is. If you are comparing quotes, ask whether assembly is inside the number or billed on top of it.

### The drop has to be right

Over a dining table, a chandelier typically hangs 30 to 36 inches above the table surface. In an entry, the bottom of the fixture should sit at least seven feet off the floor — and in a two-storey foyer it should not drop below the second-floor line of sight. Getting the chain length wrong by a foot is visible every day you live with it, and re-hanging at height costs the scaffold trip all over again. Measure it on paper before anyone climbs.

## What you can reasonably do yourself

Honest assessment, including the parts that argue against hiring anyone:

**Worth doing yourself:**
- Choosing and buying the fixture. Showroom and online prices are the same for everyone; there is no contractor discount worth routing it through us. Weigh it or note the listed weight — that number drives the box question.
- Measuring the room: ceiling height, table position, and where your eye level sits from the door and the stairs. The drop decision is yours, and it is better made before the scaffold goes up.
- Photographing the existing fixture, the switch, and — if you are comfortable — noting what is already hanging there and roughly what it weighs.

**Not worth doing yourself:**
- Hanging a heavy fixture at height. A chandelier over 50 pounds, on a box you have not verified, eighteen feet over a hard entry floor, is the combination this trade exists for. In Texas the wiring connection itself is licensed work — a fixture swap still touches it.
- Guessing at the box. If you cannot confirm the existing box is rated for the new fixture's weight, that is the job, and it belongs to the installer.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much does chandelier installation cost in Leander TX?**
For most projects, $350 to $1,250 for professional installation, around $500 on average in Angi's 2026 data — fixture separate, most fixtures running $100 to $500. A straightforward swap on an existing rated box at standard ceiling height is the low end; a two-storey entry or a new location with no wiring is what moves a job up the range and beyond it.

**How much does it cost to hang a chandelier in a two-storey entry?**
Plan on $700 to $2,000 in labour for a new high-ceiling installation, and $200 to $400 in labour for a replacement at height. The premium is access — scaffolding or tall-ladder setup and a two-person crew — plus a rated box and a carefully measured drop. Our work page shows a twenty-light chandelier installed in a two-storey entry.

**Does my chandelier need a special electrical box?**
If it is heavy, yes. The fixture hangs from the box, and fixtures over about 50 pounds need a fan-rated box or a brace anchored to the joist. Upgrading the box typically adds $100 to $200. Any installer quoting a heavy fixture should be able to tell you what box it will hang from.

**Can a chandelier go where there is no existing light?**
Yes — it is common in dining rooms built with a capped ceiling outlet and in entries where the builder's fixture is undersized. It means a new box, a wire run and a switch, which is why a new location prices at $220 to $3,400 installed instead of swap money. Whether the ceiling is open to an attic above decides most of where in that range it lands.

**How low should a chandelier hang?**
Over a dining table, 30 to 36 inches above the table. In an entry or living space, the bottom of the fixture at least seven feet from the floor — higher in a two-storey foyer, where it should stay in the upper volume of the room and read correctly from the second floor as well as the door.

**Do I need a permit to install a chandelier in Cedar Park or Leander?**
A like-for-like fixture swap on existing wiring generally does not add a permit. New wiring, a new circuit or a relocated fixture is permitted electrical work in both cities, and the licensed contractor pulls it as part of the job. Ask which case yours is — the answer should come easily.

**Can you add a dimmer for the chandelier?**
Yes, and a chandelier is the fixture that benefits most from one. Adding a dimmer switch at the same visit typically runs $100 to $200. Check the fixture's bulbs are dimmable — many LED chandeliers need a compatible dimmer, which is a five-minute conversation before the visit instead of a flicker after it.

The fixture is the part with the price tag, but it is not what sets the installation price. The box rating, the ceiling height and whether wiring already exists at the spot set it: a few hundred dollars for a swap at standard height, four figures when the job is a two-storey rigging exercise or a brand-new location. The two quotes worth being suspicious of are the one that never asked the fixture's weight, and the one that never asked the ceiling height.
