# Eggshell, Satin or Semi-Gloss: Picking a Paint Sheen You Won't Regret

Most people spend hours choosing a colour and thirty seconds choosing a sheen. Then they live with the sheen decision every day, because sheen is what decides whether the wall looks smooth, whether you can wipe it, and whether every roller mark shows at four in the afternoon.

The whole thing is one trade-off:

> **The shinier the paint, the easier it is to clean — and the more it advertises every flaw in the wall.**

That is the entire rule. Everything below is applying it.

---

## The sheens, least to most shiny

### Flat (matte)

No reflection. Light hits the surface and scatters.

**Hides the most.** Patches, roller marks, uneven texture, a wavy old wall — flat swallows all of it. This is why builders use it on ceilings and why it is the standard for repairs you want invisible.

**Cleans the worst.** Wiping a mark off older flat paint often takes some of the paint with it, leaving a burnished spot worse than the mark.

**Use it on:** ceilings, formal rooms with no traffic, adult bedrooms, walls in rough shape.
**Avoid:** hallways, kids' rooms, kitchens, bathrooms, anywhere near a door handle.

Modern "premium matte" or "scrubbable flat" lines have genuinely improved washability while keeping most of the hiding. If you want flat's look in a room that gets touched, that is the product to ask for — expect to pay more.

### Eggshell

A slight lustre — like an actual eggshell. The most common interior wall sheen in American homes, because it is the compromise that works in the most rooms.

Hides most minor flaws, takes gentle cleaning, does not look plasticky.

**Use it on:** living rooms, dining rooms, bedrooms, most general walls. If you do not want to think about it, use eggshell.

### Satin

Noticeably smoother and more reflective. A soft sheen you can see across a room.

Genuinely washable — this is where you can scrub without damaging the film. But it reflects light directionally, which means it shows surface imperfections, lap marks and repairs far more than eggshell.

**Use it on:** hallways, kids' rooms, laundry, bathrooms, kitchen walls, anywhere that gets hands on it.
**Be careful:** satin on a wall with wavy drywall or old patching will make all of it visible.

### Semi-gloss

Clearly shiny. Hard, moisture-resistant, scrubbable.

**Use it on:** trim, baseboards, casings, doors, cabinets, and bathroom walls where humidity is severe.
**Avoid on large wall areas** unless you are deliberately going for it. It looks institutional and it exposes everything.

### High gloss

Near-lacquer. Almost mirror-like.

Specialist use — a front door, a statement piece of furniture, occasionally a library. It demands a perfectly prepared surface, because it shows literally every defect. Not a wall finish for a normal house.

**The one rule most worth keeping:** trim in semi-gloss, walls in something flatter. The sheen contrast is what makes trim read as trim. Painting walls and trim in the same sheen makes a room look unfinished even when the workmanship is good.

## The mistake that ruins good work

**Putting a high sheen on a wall that cannot carry it.**

Satin or semi-gloss on a wall with old patches, a wavy surface or inconsistent texture turns every one of those into a visible feature. The paint did not cause the problem — it revealed one that flat was politely hiding.

Before going up in sheen, look at the wall with a work light held flat against it, pointing along the surface. Whatever you can see in that light, you will see in satin every afternoon.

If the wall has issues and you want washability, the answer is either to fix the wall first ([texture matching](/posts/match-drywall-texture-orange-peel-knockdown)) or to use a premium scrubbable matte instead.

**The related mistake:** repairing a wall and repainting only the patch. Higher sheens flash badly at the boundary. Prime the repair and paint corner to corner.

## Central Texas specifics

**Bathrooms need the moisture resistance more than you think.** Long hot showers plus a marginal exhaust fan gives you a lot of humidity sitting on a wall. Satin minimum; semi-gloss or a bathroom-specific product in a small bath with poor ventilation.

**Big south and west windows are unforgiving.** Low afternoon sun rakes across walls at a shallow angle and exaggerates sheen differences and surface flaws. In a west-facing living room, consider dropping a step in sheen from what you would otherwise pick.

**Hard water leaves mineral marks** on bathroom and kitchen walls. Being able to wipe them off without burnishing the paint is worth the sheen step up.

## Buying it right

- **Trim and walls are usually different products**, not just different sheens. Trim enamels level out brush marks; wall paint does not.
- **Sheen names are not standardised** across brands. One company's "satin" is another's "eggshell." Compare the actual sheen samples in the store, not the word on the can.
- **Buy enough for all coats at once**, from the same batch, and box it — mix the cans together in one bucket. Tint varies slightly between batches and it will show on a big wall.
- **Sample the sheen, not just the colour.** Paint a large swatch and look at it in daylight and lamplight. Sheen reads very differently at 2pm and 9pm.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the difference between eggshell and satin?**
Satin has more sheen. Eggshell is a very slight lustre that hides wall imperfections well and takes gentle cleaning. Satin is noticeably smoother and more reflective, scrubs properly, and shows surface flaws more. Eggshell for general living space, satin where things get touched.

**What sheen should I use on interior walls?**
Eggshell in most rooms; satin in hallways, kitchens, bathrooms, laundry and kids' rooms; flat on ceilings and in low-traffic formal spaces or on walls in poor condition.

**Is satin or semi-gloss better for bathrooms?**
Satin is enough for most bathrooms with working ventilation and looks better on a large wall. Semi-gloss for small baths with poor ventilation, or where you want maximum moisture resistance. Semi-gloss on the trim either way.

**What sheen for trim and baseboards?**
Semi-gloss is standard. It is durable, wipes clean, and the sheen contrast against flatter walls is what visually separates the trim. Satin trim is a softer contemporary look but shows scuffs more.

**Can I paint semi-gloss over eggshell?**
Yes, but scuff-sand first and prime if adhesion is doubtful. Glossier paint does not bond well to a slick surface without keying it. Going the other way — flat over gloss — also needs sanding and usually a bonding primer.

**Why does my wall look patchy after painting?**
Usually one of three things: one coat where two were needed, unequal absorption from unprimed repairs, or lap marks from letting the paint edge dry before rolling into it. All three are more visible in satin than eggshell.

**Does flat paint really not clean at all?**
Traditional flat, largely correct — wiping tends to burnish or remove paint. Modern premium matte lines are substantially better and are a reasonable choice when you want flat's look with some washability. They cost more than standard flat.

## The summary

Shinier cleans better and hides worse. That is the whole decision.

Eggshell on most walls, satin where hands and water go, flat on ceilings, semi-gloss on trim. If a wall is in rough shape, fix it or stay flat — because satin will tell everyone about it every afternoon.
