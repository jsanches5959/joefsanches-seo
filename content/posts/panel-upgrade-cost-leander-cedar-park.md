# What an Electrical Panel Upgrade Actually Costs in Leander & Cedar Park (2026 Numbers)

Search "panel upgrade cost" and you get a number around fifteen hundred dollars. Then a local electrician quotes you three or four thousand, and it looks like a markup. Usually it isn't. The two numbers are pricing two different jobs.

The cheap number is the panel itself — new breaker box, same amperage, same meter, same service wire, swapped in place. The bigger number is a full service upgrade: 100 amps to 200 amps, with a new meter base, new service-entrance conductors, grounding brought to current code, a permit, an inspection, and the utility disconnecting and reconnecting your house. Same words on the quote, very different day of work.

Here is what a panel upgrade actually costs in Leander, Cedar Park, Georgetown and greater Austin, and what decides which job yours is.

## Why the range is so wide

### Panel swap vs. full service upgrade

This is most of the spread. A panel swap reuses everything outside the box: the meter base is correctly rated, the service-entrance cable can carry the new amperage, and the grounding is already to code. That is the $1,300-to-$2,000 job for a 200-amp panel.

A service upgrade replaces that outside work too. A house on 100-amp service cannot get the benefit of a 200-amp panel until the meter base and the wire feeding it are also rated for 200 amps. Add a new meter base, mast or weatherhead, service-entrance conductors, ground rods and bonding, and the utility's disconnect and reconnect, and you have the $2,500-to-$4,500 local job. Ask every quote, in plain words: is this a panel swap, or a full service upgrade? The answer explains the price more than the brand of panel does.

### Amperage

Amperage is the headline driver in every cost guide for a reason. A 100-amp panel runs $800 to $1,500 installed, a 200-amp panel $1,300 to $2,000, and a 400-amp panel $2,000 to $4,000 — and the bigger steps also pull in heavier service wire and, at 400 amps, often twin 200-amp panels. Most Leander and Cedar Park homes that need an upgrade need 200 amps: enough for an EV charger, a hot tub, a modern kitchen and central air running at the same time. 400 amps is large homes, workshops and whole-home charging, not the default upsell.

### The panel you have now

An old panel that is simply full is a straightforward job. An old panel that is a known hazard is a different conversation. Federal Pacific Stab-Lok and Zinsco panels — still in service in older homes around Buttercup Creek, Anderson Mill and Cypress Creek — are replaced as full code-compliant jobs, typically $1,500 to $4,500, because the breakers themselves are the problem, not just the capacity. A 60-amp fuse box going to a modern 200-amp service is a rebuild, $3,500 to $6,500, because every branch circuit gets reworked and relabelled.

Newer builds in Travisso, Crystal Falls, Bryson and Twin Creeks usually already have 200-amp service. Their question is rarely amperage — it is space. A full panel with no open breaker slots for a charger or a hot tub may need a subpanel or a panel with more spaces, which is a smaller job than a service upgrade. Nobody should sell you 200 amps you already have.

### Location and moving the panel

A panel that stays where it is prices at the base range. Moving one — out of a closet, off a wall that is being remodelled, or to meet current code clearances — adds $1,500 to $4,000, because every circuit has to be rerouted to the new location. If a quote includes a move you did not ask for, ask what code issue is forcing it. Sometimes there is a real one. Sometimes there isn't.

### Permits and the utility

A panel upgrade is permitted work in Leander and Cedar Park, full stop. Budget $150 to $300 for the permit and drywall repair around the job; in Austin the electrical permit itself is listed at $185. The permit is pulled by the licensed electrician, not by you.

The utility side is the part homeowners forget to ask about. Depending on the address, the disconnect and reconnect is coordinated with Pedernales Electric Cooperative, Oncor or Austin Energy, each with its own meter and scheduling rules. The power is typically off for four to six hours on swap day, and most standard 100-to-200-amp upgrades are done in one full day. A quote that never mentions the utility has not planned the job yet.

### New circuits and code breakers

A panel upgrade is often triggered by something else — an EV charger, a hot tub, an addition, a tankless water heater. Those circuits are separate work from the panel, and current code can require AFCI or GFCI protection on circuits that did not have it, typically $200 to $800 in added breakers on a larger job. Compare quotes on whether the new circuit that started all this is actually inside the number.

## Signs the panel is the problem

- Breakers that trip repeatedly on normal use, or a breaker that will not reset
- A panel that is full — no open slots — when you need a charger, hot tub or addition circuit
- Flickering lights when the air conditioner starts, or lights that dim under load
- A Federal Pacific, Zinsco or fuse panel still in service
- A 100-amp (or 60-amp) service under a house that has added central air, a modern kitchen and an EV since it was built
- Insurance or a home sale asking about the panel by name — both happen regularly with the hazard brands above

None of those automatically means a full service upgrade. A load calculation is what decides — our licensed partner runs the numbers and tells you honestly whether the panel carries the load, needs a subpanel, or needs replacing. Nobody sells you an upgrade the math doesn't call for.

## What you can reasonably do yourself

Honest assessment, including the parts that argue against hiring anyone:

**Worth doing yourself:**
- Photographing the panel with the door open and the label readable — amperage, brand and how full it is. That photo answers half the questions in an estimate call.
- Listing what you are actually adding: charger, hot tub, addition, appliance. The load list, not the panel, is what sizes the job.
- Checking the brand. If the label says Federal Pacific or Zinsco, you already know the conversation is replacement, not capacity.

**Not worth doing yourself:**
- Replacing or upgrading the panel. In Texas this is licensed, permitted work that involves the utility disconnecting your service. It is dangerous and illegal to do it yourself, and no honest guide should soften that.
- Guessing at the load calculation. Undersizing wastes the whole job; oversizing to 400 amps wastes money you did not need to spend.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much does a panel upgrade cost in Leander TX?**
For the panel alone, plan on Angi's Austin numbers: about $1,570 on average, most homeowners paying $1,177 to $1,962, and a 200-amp panel at $1,300 to $2,000 installed. A complete 100-to-200-amp service upgrade — new meter base, service entrance, grounding, permit and utility coordination — is typically quoted at $2,500 to $4,500 by Austin-area licensed electricians. Which job yours is decides the number.

**How much does it cost to upgrade from 100 amps to 200 amps?**
The panel-and-install guides put a 200-amp upgrade at $1,300 to $2,000 when the existing meter base and service wire can be reused. When they cannot — the common case on a true 100-amp service — the all-in local range is $2,500 to $4,500. A 200-to-400-amp upgrade runs $4,000 and up. Ask whether your quote is a swap or a full service upgrade before comparing it to anyone else's.

**Do I need a panel upgrade for an EV charger?**
Sometimes, not always. A home with a modern 200-amp panel and open slots usually takes a Level 2 charger on a new dedicated circuit with no panel work — we break that job down in our EV charger cost guide. A home on 100-amp service, or with a full panel, may need a subpanel or an upgrade first. The load calculation decides, and it should be shown to you, not just asserted.

**How much does it cost to replace a Federal Pacific panel?**
Typically $1,500 to $4,500. Federal Pacific Stab-Lok and Zinsco panels are replaced as full code-compliant jobs rather than like-for-like swaps, and whether the replacement also becomes a service upgrade — new meter base and service wire — is what moves it inside that range.

**Do I need a permit for a panel upgrade in Cedar Park or Leander?**
Yes. A panel replacement or service upgrade is permitted electrical work in both cities, and the licensed electrician pulls the permit and schedules the inspection as part of the job. Budget about $150 to $300 for permit and related costs. Work offered without a permit on a service upgrade is a red flag, not a saving.

**How long does a panel upgrade take, and will my power be off?**
Most standard upgrades are one full day of work, with the power off for about four to six hours during the actual swap. The utility disconnect and reconnect — PEC, Oncor or Austin Energy depending on the address — is coordinated as part of the job so the outage is planned, not a surprise.

**Can I just add a subpanel instead of upgrading?**
Often, yes, if the service amperage is enough and the problem is only breaker space. A subpanel adds slots without replacing the service, and it is the right answer for a lot of newer 200-amp homes in Leander and Cedar Park that are full but not overloaded. If the service itself is undersized for the load, a subpanel does not fix that — it just gives an overloaded service more places to plug in.
