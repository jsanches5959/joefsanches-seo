# Home Repairs in Crystal Falls, Leander TX: What 15-to-25-Year-Old Homes Need Now

Crystal Falls opened around 2000 and covers roughly 3,000 acres and 3,600 homes across ten sub-neighborhoods. That maturity is the reason people buy here — established trees, settled landscaping, the golf course, Hill Country terrain. It is also a repair schedule. A home built in 2002 has now been through more than twenty Texas summers, and the things that wear out on a 15-to-25-year-old house wear out on roughly the same timetable in every section of the neighborhood.

## 1. Exterior paint: the 20-year wall

Production paint from the early 2000s was not applied to last two decades in Texas sun, and Crystal Falls' elevated, west-facing lots take the worst of it. The failure pattern is consistent: chalking and fading on the south and west walls first, then caulk joints opening at the trim, then water getting behind the siding edges. Once the caulk line goes, the paint is no longer the problem — the water is.

The order matters more than the brand of paint. Patch and repair the drywall and siding damage first, re-caulk every joint, prime the bare spots, then two coats. Our [exterior paint guide](/posts/exterior-paint-texas-heat-how-long-lasts) covers how long a good job lasts in this heat, and the [Leander painting page](/services/interior-exterior-painting-leander-tx) covers what a full repaint includes. Golf-course and view lots get one extra note: overspray discipline matters when the neighbor's yard is a fairway.

## 2. Drywall cracks on sloped lots

Crystal Falls' Hill Country terrain is the selling point and the crack source. Homes on sloped lots sit on cut-and-fill ground over limestone and clay, and that ground moves a little every wet-dry cycle. The result is the classic pattern: a diagonal crack over a doorway, a seam line reappearing in the same ceiling joint, nail pops along one wall.

Most of it is cosmetic movement, not foundation failure — our [drywall cracks guide](/posts/drywall-cracks-when-to-worry-central-texas) walks through which cracks are which. The repair that lasts is cut back to solid material, re-tape, float, and match the existing texture (orange peel and knockdown are the two Crystal Falls standards) before paint. A patch that skips the texture match is more visible than the crack was. Details and pricing approach are on the [Leander drywall repair page](/services/drywall-repair-leander-tx).

## 3. The mature oaks: trimming season is now

The established live oaks are a third of what a Crystal Falls lot is worth, and at 20-plus years they are big trees now. Two rules run this work:

- **Timing.** Oak wilt spreads through fresh cuts during the high-risk months. The safe window runs July through January — which means right now, late fall and winter, is exactly when Crystal Falls oak work should happen. Our [live oak trimming guide](/posts/live-oak-trimming-central-texas) covers raising, thinning and reducing, and what never to do to an oak.
- **Removal is the last resort, priced by size.** Removal in the Austin area averages about $736, with most jobs between $256 and $1,962 depending on height and access — sloped, fenced golf-course lots with no alley access sit at the top of the range because everything comes out through the gate by hand. The [Leander tree page](/tree-removal-leander-tx) has the full breakdown.

One HOA note: Crystal Falls runs a master HOA plus sub-neighborhood HOAs in some sections, and tree removal — especially of a mature oak — is the kind of work worth a quick check against your section's rules before the saw starts, not after a letter arrives.

## 4. Decks and patios on moving ground

Original-era decks in Crystal Falls are at end of life: sun-split boards on the south side, fasteners working loose as the clay moves under the footings, rot where the ledger meets the house. A deck with a sound frame can be resurfaced — new boards over good bones. A deck whose framing or footings have failed is a rebuild, and pretending otherwise just moves the cost to next year with interest.

New builds run $30–$50 per square foot installed (most projects $3,946–$10,909); repairs typically run $852–$3,345. Sloped lots add footing and drainage work that flat-lot quotes never mention, which is why the walk-through comes before the number. The [Leander deck and patio page](/services/deck-patio-builder-leander-tx) covers materials and the HOA/permit step — most structural outdoor work here needs an HOA submittal first.

## 5. Floors: the carpet is done

Twenty-year-old carpet and builder-grade laminate are the most common interior refresh in the neighborhood, usually paired with the repaint. LVP at $3–$8 per square foot installed is the default replacement in family homes — waterproof, hard on scratches, and honest over a slab. The part that decides whether the job looks right in five years is invisible: slab levelling and subfloor repair before the new floor goes down, especially in the sloped sections where the slab edge has moved. Our [Leander flooring page](/services/flooring-installation-leander-tx) covers the full process, tear-out included.

## 6. Pressure washing: the cheapest reset

Limestone dust, hard-water spotting and mildew on shaded north walls build up slowly enough that most owners stop seeing them. A driveway runs $100–$250 and a full house wash $186–$459, most homes around $300 — the highest-return few hours on any pre-listing punch list, and the fastest way to make a 2004 house read as maintained. Soft washing for siding, higher pressure for concrete. The [Leander pressure washing page](/services/pressure-washing-leander-tx) has the details.

## What order to do it in

1. **Water first.** Caulk, paint failure, roof-edge and leak stains — anything letting water in outranks everything cosmetic.
2. **Trees in the safe window.** Oak work between July and January, while the window is open.
3. **Structure and movement.** Deck framing, then drywall cracks (after you have watched them through a season, if they are new).
4. **Surfaces.** Paint, then floors, then washing as the finishing pass.

## Frequently asked questions

**What breaks first in a 20-year-old Crystal Falls home?**
The exterior caulk and paint system goes first — south and west walls chalk, fade and open at the joints after 15 to 20 Texas summers. After that it is the wear items on the same clock: original carpet and laminate, deck boards and fasteners, and water heaters and HVAC, which are mechanical systems your inspector will date for you. The structure itself, on a maintained home, is rarely the problem.

**How much does it cost to repaint a Crystal Falls house?**
Interior rooms run $350 to $650 for a bedroom, walls only, and a whole 2,000 sq ft interior runs $4,000 to $8,000 for walls or $6,500 to $12,000 with ceilings and trim. Exterior pricing depends on size, stories, siding condition and how much caulk and repair work the prep uncovers, so it is quoted as a written estimate after a walk-through rather than a per-foot guess.

**Are the drywall cracks in my Crystal Falls home a foundation problem?**
Usually not. Sloped lots over limestone and clay move slightly with every wet and dry cycle, and diagonal cracks over doorways and reappearing ceiling seams are the normal signature of that movement. Cracks that are wide, growing fast, offset from one side to the other, or paired with doors suddenly sticking deserve a foundation specialist's look before any cosmetic repair — patching a live structural crack just schedules the same repair twice.

**When should I trim my Crystal Falls live oaks?**
Between July and January. Oak wilt spreads through fresh pruning cuts most easily in the spring high-risk period, so late fall and winter are the safe working window in Central Texas. Trimming runs about $245 to $1,029 depending on size and canopy, and cuts should be painted immediately as part of the job.

**Do I need HOA approval for repairs in Crystal Falls?**
For like-for-like repairs, generally no. For anything that changes the exterior look or footprint — a new or rebuilt deck, a patio cover, exterior color changes, removing a mature tree — expect a submittal to the master HOA or your sub-neighborhood HOA first. We flag which applies during the walk-through so the approval is in hand before work starts, not after a letter arrives.
