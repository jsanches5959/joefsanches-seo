# Home Repairs in Rancho Sienna, Leander TX: The First-Cycle List for Newer Master-Planned Homes

Rancho Sienna is a master-planned community developed by Century Communities on the Leander and Liberty Hill border - located in Liberty Hill, TX, off Ronald Reagan Blvd near the 183A corridor, and served by Liberty Hill ISD, not Leander ISD, which is the first thing buyers and owners here need straight. Homes run from the mid-$300s to the upper $500s, from smaller starter homes in the 1,600 to 2,000 sq ft range to larger family homes pushing 3,500-plus sq ft, with open-concept layouts and covered patios. The amenity center, resort-style pool, splash pad, playgrounds, walking trails and event lawn are run by an active HOA, and like most master-planned communities in this area the community carries MUD taxes on top of standard county taxes. Downtown Austin is roughly 35 to 45 minutes via 183A and the Domain about 25 to 30.

That still-building status is the point. Century Communities still has active new construction in Rancho Sienna, so the community runs on two clocks at once: the newest sections sit under builder warranty, while the earliest homes are entering their first real repair cycle - builder paint and caulk at the end of their run in Texas sun, settling cracks showing up as the ground settles in, and builder carpet wearing out. The Hill Country setting, with more dramatic topography than the flatter Leander communities, adds slope and drainage to the list. This is the Rancho Sienna list we see, what each item costs in the area, and the order to tackle it in.

## 1. Exterior paint and caulk: the builder-grade first cycle

Rancho Sienna's earliest homes carry what every newer master-planned build carries: builder-grade flat paint that was ready for a real color the day it went on, and a caulk system now at the end of its first run. The south and west elevations fail first in Central Texas sun, caulk joints open at the trim and around windows, and green film builds on north-facing siding in the shaded sections. Left alone, open joints are how water gets behind the elevation - a paint job becomes a repair job.

The order matters more than the brand: repair first, then caulk, prime and paint. Our [Georgetown painting page](/services/interior-exterior-painting-georgetown-tx) covers what a full repaint includes, and our [exterior paint guide](/posts/exterior-paint-texas-heat-how-long-lasts) covers how long a good job lasts in this heat. One Rancho Sienna note: an exterior color change needs HOA approval first, even when the new color is close to the old one - get the approval in hand before the crew, not after. A repaint in the same color usually does not need it.

## 2. Settling cracks and nail pops in newer construction

A new subdivision's ground gets graded, filled and built on fast, and the first years of wet-dry cycles over limestone and clay show up inside as the classic pattern: a diagonal crack over a doorway, a ceiling seam that reappears in the same joint, nail pops along one wall. In a newer home this is almost always cosmetic settling, not foundation failure - our [drywall cracks guide](/posts/drywall-cracks-when-to-worry-central-texas) walks through which cracks are which. The cracks that earn a specialist's look first are the ones that are wide, offset from one side to the other, growing fast, or paired with doors that suddenly stick.

The repair that lasts is cut back to solid material, re-tape, float, and match the existing texture before paint. Rancho Sienna interiors lean smooth and modern, which is less forgiving than orange peel: a patch that skips the texture and sheen match shows every time the afternoon sun comes across the wall. Details are on the [Georgetown drywall page](/services/drywall-repair-georgetown-tx).

## 3. The warranty clock: two communities in one

The national builders in communities like this carry the standard structure: one year on workmanship, two on systems, ten on structure. In the sections still building out, the move is to build the punch list before each window closes and get it in front of the builder while it is still their bill - new builds come with builder warranties, but only for the items you actually file. In the earliest sections, those first windows have already closed, and anything that looks structural - cracks that are wide, offset, or growing, doors that suddenly stick - is worth a specialist's look while you still know which side of the 10-year line you are on. Once the warranty is gone, the small stuff becomes a handyman list: a door that will not latch, a bathroom fan that rattles, baseboard that came loose, caulk and grout lines that have opened up. ## 5. West-facing yards, builder trees, patios and drainage

West-facing yards in Rancho Sienna bake all afternoon, and that sun decides a lot of the outdoor list. Covered patios are standard or a common upgrade in these floor plans, and the structures that extend them - decks, patio covers, pergolas - live or die on the parts you stop being able to see once they are finished: footings set right in thin soil over limestone, framing, fasteners, drainage. New decks run $30-$50 per square foot, most projects $3,946-$10,909; repairs run $852-$3,345. The topography that makes the community feel like Hill Country cuts the other way on drainage: on sloped lots, water should move away from the slab and off the lot, not pond against the house after a heavy rain, and that gets a hard look before any deck or fence money goes in.

The trees run on Central Texas rules:

- **Timing.** The fast-growing builder trees planted across Rancho Sienna - red oaks, Arizona ash, hackberries - are now big enough to hang over roofs, and the oaks among them run on the oak-wilt calendar. Oak wilt spreads through fresh cuts during the high-risk months, so the safe window runs July through January - which means right now, late fall and winter, is when oak work should happen. Our [live oak trimming guide](/posts/live-oak-trimming-central-texas) covers raising, thinning and reducing, and what never to do to an oak.
- **Removal is the last resort, priced by size.** Removal in the Austin area averages about $736, with most jobs between $256 and $1,962 depending on height and access, and a large tree can need a permit and HOA approval first - we flag which applies before quoting. Wood fences that went in with the first closings are on the same clock as the paint: pickets cupping and graying, gates sagging as posts move in clay. A sound fence can be repaired section by section; failed posts are the rebuild trigger.

## What order to do it in

1. **Water first.** Caulk, open joints, drainage running toward the house - anything letting water in outranks everything cosmetic, and sloped lots get this look first.
2. **Warranty-window items.** In the still-building sections, anything that might be the builder's bill gets listed and filed before its window closes; in the earliest sections, check which side of the 10-year structural line you are on.
3. **Trees in the safe window.** Oak work between July and January, while the window is open.
4. **Surfaces.** Paint, then floors if the builder carpet is going, then washing as the finishing pass.

## Frequently asked questions

**What breaks first in a newer Rancho Sienna home?**
The exterior paint and caulk system and the builder carpet, in most cases - builder-grade flat paint fades and caulk joints open at the end of their first run in Central Texas sun, and builder carpet wears out on the traffic lines well before the house does. After that it is the settling signature of newer construction: nail pops, seam cracks and trim joints opened by clay movement. The structure itself, on a maintained home, is rarely the problem.

**How much does it cost to repaint a Rancho Sienna house?**
Interior rooms run $350 to $650 for a bedroom, walls only, and a whole 2,000 sq ft interior runs $4,000 to $8,000 for walls or $6,500 to $12,000 with ceilings and trim. Exterior pricing depends on size, stories, elevation materials and how much caulk and crack repair the prep uncovers, so it is quoted as a written estimate after a walk-through rather than a per-foot guess.

**Are the drywall cracks in my Rancho Sienna home a foundation problem?**
Usually not. Lots over limestone and clay move slightly with every wet and dry cycle, and in newer construction the diagonal cracks over doorways, reappearing ceiling seams and nail pops are the normal signature of that movement as the ground settles in. Cracks that are wide, growing fast, offset from one side to the other, or paired with doors suddenly sticking deserve a foundation specialist's look before any cosmetic repair - and if the home is still inside its structural warranty window, file it with the builder first.

**When should I trim my Rancho Sienna oaks?**
Between July and January. Oak wilt spreads through fresh pruning cuts most easily in the spring high-risk period, so late fall and winter are the safe working window in Central Texas. Trimming runs about $245 to $1,029 depending on size and canopy, and cuts should be painted immediately as part of the job. The builder trees planted across the community are now big enough to hang over roofs, so the scope is real canopy work, not a street-tree trim.

**Does Rancho Sienna have an HOA I need approval from for repairs?**
Yes - Rancho Sienna has an active HOA, and an exterior color change needs HOA architectural approval before work starts, even when the new color is close to the old one. A repaint in the same color usually does not. Exterior changes, additions and anything that alters the footprint or elevation usually need an HOA architectural submittal first, and removing a large tree can need HOA approval as well. We flag which applies during the walk-through and get any needed approval in hand before work starts. One more Rancho Sienna specific: the community is served by Liberty Hill ISD, not Leander ISD, and carries MUD taxes - neither changes a repair, but both are worth having straight before you buy or sell here.
