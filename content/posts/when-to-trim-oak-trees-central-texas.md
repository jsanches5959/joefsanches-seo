# When to Trim Oak Trees in Central Texas (Oak Wilt Rules That Matter)

If you have oaks around Leander, Cedar Park or Austin, *when* you trim them matters as much as *how*. Prune at the wrong time and you can expose the tree, and your whole neighborhood's oaks, to oak wilt, one of the most destructive tree diseases in Texas.

## The rule

The Texas A&M Forest Service recommends:

- **Avoid pruning or wounding oaks from February 1 through June 30.**
- **Paint every cut or wound on an oak immediately,** in any season, with wound dressing or latex paint.

That means the **safest window for oak trimming is July through January**, with the coolest months the most comfortable time for the work.

## Why spring is the danger zone

Oak wilt is a fungus. In red oaks (Texas red oak, Shumard oak), it forms spore mats under the bark that attract small sap-feeding beetles. In late winter and spring, those beetles are most active, and they're drawn to fresh wounds on oaks. A beetle carrying spores can infect a healthy tree through a fresh pruning cut.

Once a tree is infected, the disease can spread underground through connected root systems. **Live oaks** are especially vulnerable because they often share roots with neighboring live oaks, so one infected tree can lead to a spreading patch of dead and dying trees.

## Live oaks vs. red oaks

- **Live oaks** are the most common shade trees in Leander and Cedar Park yards. They can survive infection for a while, but losses are common and the disease spreads tree to tree through roots.
- **Red oaks** (Texas red oak, Shumard) usually die quickly once infected, and they're the ones that produce the spore mats that spread the disease.

Both should follow the same pruning rules.

## What if I must cut an oak in spring?

Sometimes you have to: a storm-broken limb, a hazard over the roof, or a branch hitting a power line. In that case:

- make the cut cleanly,
- **paint it right away,**
- don't leave broken, torn limbs untreated.

Emergency work in spring is fine as long as wounds are sealed immediately.

## Signs of oak wilt

Call for a closer look if you see:

- **Live oaks:** leaves with yellow or brown veins (veinal necrosis), leaves dropping in spring or summer, thinning canopy, or several nearby live oaks declining.
- **Red oaks:** leaves turning dull green, bronze or red from the edges inward, often quickly, in late spring or summer.

Diagnosis can require testing. The Texas A&M Forest Service has resources and specialists for oak wilt in Central Texas.

## Good oak pruning, any time of year

- **Don't top oaks.** Topping (cutting the whole crown back) creates big wounds and weak regrowth.
- **Remove dead, crossing and rubbing limbs** first.
- **Raise the canopy** for clearance over the roof, driveway and sidewalk instead of cutting the tree's natural spread. See [live oak trimming](/posts/live-oak-trimming-central-texas).
- **Don't take more than about a quarter of the live canopy** in one season.
- **Clean tools** between trees.

## Frequently asked questions

**What months should you not trim oak trees in Texas?**
The Texas A&M Forest Service recommends avoiding pruning or wounding oaks from February 1 through June 30.

**When is the best time to trim live oaks in Central Texas?**
July through January. The cooler months, roughly late fall through January, are the most comfortable time for larger pruning jobs.

**Do I need to paint oak tree cuts?**
Yes. Paint every cut or wound on an oak right away, in every season, to help keep oak wilt beetles away from fresh wounds.

**Can oak wilt spread from my neighbor's yard?**
Yes. Live oaks often share root systems, so oak wilt can move underground from tree to tree across property lines.
