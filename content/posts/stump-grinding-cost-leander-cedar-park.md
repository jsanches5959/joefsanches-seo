# Stump Grinding Cost in Leander & Cedar Park (And When It's Worth It)

The tree is gone, but the stump is still there, catching the mower, sprouting suckers, or sitting right where you wanted a flower bed. Here's what it costs to get rid of it and which option makes sense.

## Ballpark prices

Rough planning ranges, not quotes:

| Stump | Typical range |
|---|---|
| Small (under about 12 in. across) | $100 – $200 |
| Medium (12–24 in.) | $175 – $350 |
| Large (24 in.+), live oak, or cluster | $300 – $600+ |
| Each additional stump, same visit | Often discounted |

Most tree services charge a minimum per visit, so grinding three stumps at once costs much less per stump than one at a time.

## Grinding vs. full stump removal

**Stump grinding** uses a machine to chew the stump down to several inches below the soil. The roots stay in the ground and break down over time. It's faster, cheaper and leaves a much smaller mess. This is what most people want.

**Full stump removal** means digging out the stump and the main root ball. It leaves a big hole, takes heavy equipment and costs considerably more. You'd only do it if you're building, pouring a slab or putting in a pool right where the stump is.

## What drives the price

- **Diameter.** The wider the stump, the longer the grind.
- **Species.** Live oak and mesquite are hard and slow. Hackberry and ash grind quickly.
- **Surface roots.** Oaks and cedar elms often have big roots running across the yard. Grinding those adds time.
- **Access.** A grinder needs a path in. Narrow side gates (common in newer Leander subdivisions) may require a smaller machine.
- **Rock.** Much of western Williamson County sits on shallow limestone. Rock right around the stump slows grinding and wears out teeth, and it can limit how deep the grinder can go.

## What happens after grinding

You're left with a pile of wood chips and soil roughly where the stump was. You can:

- **Use the grindings as mulch** around other plants.
- **Have them hauled away** and fill the hole with topsoil to plant grass or a bed.

Expect the spot to settle a bit over the next few months as buried wood breaks down. Top it up with soil if needed.

## Can I just leave the stump?

Sometimes, yes. Leaving a stump is fine if it's:

- in a natural area or back corner,
- cut low enough not to be a trip or mower hazard,
- not sprouting new shoots.

Reasons to grind it anyway:

- **Suckers.** Some species (cedar elm, hackberry, chinaberry, privet) send up new sprouts from the stump and roots.
- **Termites and pests.** A rotting stump next to the house can attract wood-loving insects.
- **Mowing and looks.** A stump in the middle of the lawn is a constant obstacle.
- **Planning.** If you want to replant in the same area, grinding is the way to clear it.

## Before grinding: call 811

Texas law requires calling 811 before digging so utilities can mark buried lines, and grinding goes below grade. A tree service should handle this, but it's worth asking.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much does stump grinding cost in Cedar Park?**
Most residential stumps fall between about $100 and $400, depending on diameter, species, roots and access. Multiple stumps in one visit lower the cost per stump.

**How deep does stump grinding go?**
Typically several inches below the soil, enough to plant grass over it. Shallow limestone common around Leander and Cedar Park can limit depth in some spots.

**Will the roots keep growing after the stump is ground?**
For most species, no. The roots decay over time. A few species that sucker heavily may send up sprouts for a season, which can be cut back.

**Can I plant a new tree in the same spot?**
It's better to plant a few feet away. The buried wood takes years to break down and can tie up nitrogen in the soil.
