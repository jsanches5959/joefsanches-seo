# Home Repairs in Larkspur, Leander TX: The First-Cycle List for 2016-and-Newer Homes

Larkspur is the boutique entry in Leander's master-planned lineup: a smaller, intentionally designed community in central Leander, just east of 183A near the Crystal Falls Parkway retail corridor, built primarily by David Weekley Homes. Where the mega-developments sell scale, Larkspur sells streetscape - alley-loaded garages, real front porches, tree-lined streets and pocket parks, with a community pool and playgrounds. Homes run about $400,000 to $580,000, zoned to Leander ISD - Pleasant Hill Elementary, Leander Middle School, and Leander High School - and the HOA runs about $85 a month.

The homes were built from 2016 on, which puts the earliest ones at the same turning point as every other first-generation Leander neighborhood: the first real repair cycle. Builder paint and caulk are at the end of their run in Texas sun, the porches that define these streets take weather on every face, alley-loaded garage doors have done a decade of daily duty as the family's real front door, and the street trees planted at build are canopy for the first time. ## 1. The front porch: Larkspur's signature takes the weather first

The front porch is the whole design idea in Larkspur, and it is also the most exposed wood and paint on the house. Porch ceilings fade and peel, painted porch floors wear through on the traffic line to the front door, and column bases wick water at grade and soften. On a 2016 to 2019 home, that system is due for its first full refresh: scrape and repair, re-caulk the joints, prime the bare spots, then two coats. Left alone, a soft column base turns a paint job into a carpentry job.

The order matters more than the brand of paint. Repair any rot or siding damage first, then caulk, prime and paint. Our [exterior paint guide](/posts/exterior-paint-texas-heat-how-long-lasts) covers how long a good job lasts in this heat, and the [Leander painting page](/services/interior-exterior-painting-leander-tx) covers what a full repaint includes. One Larkspur note: exterior color changes need HOA approval first - get the approval in hand before the crew, not after.

## 2. Alley-loaded garages: the door that never rests

In an alley-loaded plan, the garage door is the family's real front door - it cycles more times a day than the front door ever will, and the alley face of the house takes sun and sprinkler spray that the street face never sees. The first-cycle list on that side of the house: door panels chalking and fading, weather seal split at the bottom, trim rot at the alley elevation, and fence gates along the alley sagging as posts move in clay. Panel repaint and seal replacement are cheap now; a rotted jamb is not. Alley drainage is worth one look while you are back there - water that ponds against the slab edge in the alley shows up later as the cracks in item 3.

## 3. Settling cracks in newer construction

A new subdivision's ground gets graded, filled and built on fast, and the first decade of wet-dry cycles over limestone and clay shows up inside as the classic pattern: a diagonal crack over a doorway, a ceiling seam that reappears in the same joint, nail pops along one wall. In a home under 10 years old this is almost always cosmetic settling, not foundation failure - our [drywall cracks guide](/posts/drywall-cracks-when-to-worry-central-texas) walks through which cracks are which.

The repair that lasts is cut back to solid material, re-tape, float, and match the existing texture before paint. Larkspur interiors lean smooth and high-finish, which is less forgiving than orange peel: a patch that skips the texture and sheen match shows every time the afternoon sun comes across the wall. Details are on the [Leander drywall repair page](/services/drywall-repair-leander-tx).

## 4. The warranty clock: the earliest homes are at the line

David Weekley builds carried the standard structure: one year on workmanship, two on systems, ten on structure. Larkspur's earliest homes, built in 2016, reach the end of that 10-year structural window right about now - and anything that looks structural - cracks that are wide, offset, or growing, doors that suddenly stick - is worth a specialist's look while you still know which side of the warranty line you are on. In the sections still building out, the move runs the other way: build the punch list before each window closes - sticky latches, grout cracks, trim gaps, settling nail pops - and get it in front of the builder while it is still their bill. Once the warranty is gone, the small stuff becomes a handyman list. Our [Leander handyman page](/services/handyman-services-leander-tx) covers how that work is priced - by the job, in half-day blocks, not by the hour.

## 5. Builder carpet out, hard floors in

Larkspur draws move-up buyers and young families, and builder carpet in a high-traffic family home is usually done well before the house is. The common upgrade is LVP through the main living areas: $3 to $8 per square foot installed, with the average Austin flooring project at $1,595 to $5,069. On newer slabs the prep point is levelling - a floor that telegraphs every slab joint looks cheap in a home this new, so the flush-transition and levelling work is part of the job, not an extra. ## 6. Street trees, fences and the wash

Larkspur's tree-lined streets were planted at build, so the oldest oaks are real canopy for the first time - and due for their first structural trim. The rules:

- **Timing.** Oak wilt spreads through fresh cuts during the high-risk months. The safe window runs July through January - which means right now, late fall and winter, is when Larkspur oak work should happen. Our [live oak trimming guide](/posts/live-oak-trimming-central-texas) covers raising, thinning and reducing, and what never to do to an oak.
- **Removal is the last resort, priced by size.** Removal in the Austin area averages about $736, with most jobs between $256 and $1,962 depending on height and access. The [Leander tree page](/tree-removal-leander-tx) has the full breakdown.

The wood fences that went in with the first closings are on the same clock: pickets cupping and graying, gates sagging as posts move in clay. A sound fence can be repaired and restained section by section; failed posts are the rebuild trigger. Check the HOA standards before changing a fence line or finish - Larkspur's streetscape is the product, and it is enforced. And the cheapest reset of all is a wash: limestone dust, hard-water spotting and mildew on shaded walls build up slowly enough that owners stop seeing them. A driveway runs $100-$250 and a full house wash $186-$459, most homes around $300. The [Leander pressure washing page](/services/pressure-washing-leander-tx) has the details.

## What order to do it in

1. **Water first.** Caulk, porch and trim rot, drainage running toward the house - anything letting water in outranks everything cosmetic.
2. **Warranty-window items.** The earliest Larkspur homes are at the 10-year structural line now; anything that might be the builder's bill gets listed and checked before its window closes.
3. **Trees in the safe window.** Oak work between July and January, while the window is open.
4. **Surfaces.** Paint, then floors if the builder carpet is going, then washing as the finishing pass.

## Frequently asked questions

**What breaks first in a 2016 to 2019 Larkspur home?**
The exterior paint and caulk system goes first, and on Larkspur homes the front porch shows it earliest - porch ceilings, porch floors and column bases take weather on every face. After that it is the wear items on the same clock: alley garage door seals and trim, builder carpet, fence pickets and gates, and the small settling cracks over doorways. The structure itself, on a maintained home, is rarely the problem.

**How much does it cost to repaint a Larkspur house?**
Interior rooms run $350 to $650 for a bedroom, walls only, and a whole 2,000 sq ft interior runs $4,000 to $8,000 for walls or $6,500 to $12,000 with ceilings and trim. Exterior pricing depends on size, stories, elevation materials and how much caulk and repair work the prep uncovers, so it is quoted as a written estimate after a walk-through rather than a per-foot guess. Exterior color changes also need HOA approval in Larkspur.

**Are the drywall cracks in my Larkspur home a foundation problem?**
Usually not. Newer lots over limestone and clay move slightly with every wet and dry cycle, and diagonal cracks over doorways and reappearing ceiling seams are the normal signature of that movement. Cracks that are wide, growing fast, offset from one side to the other, or paired with doors suddenly sticking deserve a foundation specialist's look before any cosmetic repair - and on a 2016 home, check the builder's 10-year structural warranty line first, because that window is closing now.

**When should I trim my Larkspur live oaks?**
Between July and January. Oak wilt spreads through fresh pruning cuts most easily in the spring high-risk period, so late fall and winter are the safe working window in Central Texas. Trimming runs about $245 to $1,029 depending on size and canopy, and cuts should be painted immediately as part of the job.

**Do I need HOA approval for repairs in Larkspur?**
For like-for-like repairs, generally no. For anything that changes the exterior look or footprint - exterior color changes, a new or rebuilt deck, changing a fence line, removing a mature street tree - expect a submittal to the Larkspur HOA first. We flag which applies during the walk-through and get the approval in hand before work starts.
