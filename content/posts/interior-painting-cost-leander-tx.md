# Interior Painting Cost in Leander and Cedar Park: Room-by-Room Numbers

Two painters look at the same living room. One quotes $600, one quotes $1,400. Both are being honest.

The difference is almost never the paint. It is how much preparation is in the number, how many coats, and whether trim and ceilings are included. Most homeowners compare the totals and pick the lower one, then find out what was missing.

Here is what interior painting actually costs in Leander, Cedar Park, Georgetown and greater Austin — and how to read a bid so you are comparing the same job.

## Why per-square-foot quotes mislead

You will see "$2 to $6 per square foot" quoted online. The problem is that square footage measures the easy part.

Rolling paint onto an open wall is fast. A two-person crew can cover a lot of wall in an hour. What takes the day is everything else:

- Moving and covering furniture
- Masking trim, outlets, floors and ceilings
- Filling nail holes and patching damage
- Caulking gaps at trim and corners
- Spot-priming stains and repairs
- Cutting in by hand at every edge
- Cleaning up and putting the room back

Two rooms of identical size can differ by a factor of two because one has smooth walls, one accent colour and nothing on them, and the other has twelve nail holes, a dark red feature wall going to pale grey, crown moulding, and a bookcase that has to be moved.

**Prep is the price.** Square footage is a rough proxy at best.

## What actually drives your number

### Colour change

Going from off-white to a similar off-white is one coat. Going from dark to light is two coats plus a primer, sometimes three. Going from light to a saturated colour — deep blue, true red — is a tinted primer plus two or three coats, because strong pigments have poor hide.

Red and deep yellow are the notorious ones. If a bid for a red accent wall does not mention a tinted primer, ask.

### Wall condition

Every nail hole, patch, crack and scuff is hand work before a brush touches the wall. A rental turnover with fifty picture holes is a different job from a two-year-old house.

If there is real damage, that is drywall work priced separately — see [what drywall repair costs](/posts/drywall-repair-cost-leander-cedar-park).

### Ceilings

A flat white ceiling is straightforward. Add height, and you add staging. A two-storey entry needs scaffolding or a ladder rig, and it slows everything down.

Popcorn ceilings are their own category — painting one is messy and can loosen the texture, and the honest answer is often to scrape and refinish first.

### Trim, doors and baseboards

Usually quoted separately because it is slow, exacting brush work. Trim is often a different sheen from walls — semi-gloss against eggshell — which means a separate cut and separate dry time.

A house with heavy crown moulding, wainscoting and six-panel doors has far more trim linear footage than the floor plan suggests.

### Paint quality

The gap between contractor-grade and premium paint is real: better hide (fewer coats), better scrub resistance, better colour retention. The material difference across a whole house might be $400 to $900. The labour is identical either way, so cheap paint that needs a third coat costs more, not less.

Ask which product and which line. "Sherwin-Williams" is a company; the line within it matters.

### Furniture and occupancy

An empty house is faster and cheaper. If the crew is working around furniture, kids and a home office, that is time.

**The single biggest saving available to you:** paint between tenants, before you move in, or before furniture arrives.

## How to read a bid

Bids are only comparable if they specify the same work. Ask every painter these:

1. **How many coats?** "Two coats" should be in writing. "Until it looks good" means one coat if one coat covers.
2. **Is primer included, and where?** On patches, stains, bare drywall, or everywhere?
3. **Are ceilings included?** Often excluded by default.
4. **Is trim included?** Baseboards, casings, doors, crown — itemised.
5. **What product and line?**
6. **What prep is included?** Nail holes, caulking, sanding glossy trim, patching?
7. **Who moves the furniture?**
8. **How are drywall repairs handled** if damage turns up behind a picture?
9. **Is there a warranty?** One to three years on workmanship is common.
10. **Insurance?** Ask for the certificate. A painter working on a ladder in your stairwell without liability cover is your problem, not theirs.

A bid that is 40 percent lower than the others is almost always one coat, no primer, minimal prep, or no trim. That is a legitimate service — it is just not the same service.

## Where the money is well spent and where it is not

**Worth paying for:**
- **Two coats.** One-coat jobs look fine on day one and show wear within a year.
- **Proper caulking** at trim. It is the difference between "painted" and "finished."
- **Good paint in kitchens, bathrooms and hallways** — the surfaces that get scrubbed.
- **Cutting in by hand.** Taped lines are faster and rarely as crisp.

**Not worth paying for:**
- **Premium paint on ceilings.** Nobody scrubs a ceiling.
- **Painting rooms that do not need it** to hit a whole-house price. Do the rooms you actually use.
- **Colour consultation**, usually. Paint stores do it free and sample pots cost a few dollars.

**Always do this:** buy sample pots and paint large swatches on at least two walls. Look at them in morning light, afternoon light and lamplight. Central Texas sun is intense and drags warm colours further warm than the chip suggested. A colour chosen under fluorescent store lighting is a coin flip.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much does it cost to paint a 12×12 bedroom?**
In Central Texas, roughly $350 to $650 for walls only with normal prep and two coats. Add trim and ceiling and expect $550 to $1,000. Heavy patching, a dark-to-light colour change, or high ceilings push it up.

**How much to paint a 2,000 square foot house interior?**
Walls only, typically $4,000 to $8,000. Including ceilings and trim, $6,500 to $12,000. Empty and freshly built sits at the bottom of the range; occupied with significant repairs sits at the top.

**Is it cheaper to paint before moving in?**
Considerably. An empty house removes furniture moving, protection and working-around time, and lets a crew work continuously. If you can paint in the gap between closing and move-in, do.

**How long does it take to paint a house interior?**
A single room is usually a day. A 2,000 square foot interior runs three to five working days for a small crew, longer with ceilings and trim. Drying time between coats sets the floor.

**Do painters repair drywall first?**
Most handle small holes and minor patching as part of prep. Larger damage, cracks needing re-taping, or texture matching is usually separate work — and it should happen before painting, not after.

**What sheen should I use on walls?**
Eggshell for most living spaces, satin where it needs scrubbing, flat on ceilings. Full breakdown in our [paint sheen guide](/posts/paint-sheen-guide-eggshell-satin-semigloss).

**Should I paint the ceiling the same colour as the walls?**
Flat white ceilings are the default because they bounce light and hide imperfections. Painting a ceiling in the wall colour at a lighter tint can work well in a bedroom; in a room with an uneven ceiling it will draw the eye to every flaw.
