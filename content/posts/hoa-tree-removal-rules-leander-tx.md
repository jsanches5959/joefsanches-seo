# HOA Tree Removal Rules in Leander & Cedar Park: How to Get Approved

In most of Leander's and Cedar Park's master-planned neighborhoods, the HOA has more say over your trees than the city does. Removing a tree without approval can mean a violation letter, a fine, or being told to plant a replacement at your own cost.

The good news: approval is usually simple if you ask first and give the committee what it needs.

## What most HOAs require

Every HOA's rules are different, so read your deed restrictions (CC&Rs) and architectural guidelines. Common requirements:

- **An architectural review (ARC) request** before removing any tree, or any tree over a certain size.
- **Stricter rules for front-yard trees**, since they're visible from the street. Many HOAs require a minimum number of front-yard trees.
- **A replacement tree**, often from an approved species list and minimum trunk size.
- **Approved contractors or proof of a professional service** for larger removals.
- **Stump grinding** so the yard doesn't look unfinished.

## How to get approved quickly

1. **Find the form.** It's usually on your HOA's resident portal or management company website, under architectural or modification requests.
2. **Explain why.** Dead, diseased, storm-damaged, damaging the foundation or roof, or interfering with lines are reasons committees understand.
3. **Attach photos.** Show the whole tree, the trunk and the problem (dead canopy, split trunk, lean, roots lifting the driveway).
4. **Include a contractor's note or quote** describing the tree and its condition.
5. **Say what you'll replant**, if your HOA requires it. Choosing from their approved list speeds things up.

Committees often meet monthly, so submit early. For a tree that's an immediate hazard, ask the management company about emergency approval.

## Dead or dangerous trees

Most HOAs approve dead or hazardous tree removal quickly, and some don't require approval at all for a clearly dead tree. Still document it with dated photos before and after, so there's no question later.

After a major storm, HOAs typically relax the process for downed and split trees. Take photos first, make it safe, then file the paperwork.

## Trimming vs. removal

Routine trimming usually doesn't need approval. Heavy cutting that dramatically changes a tree's shape sometimes does, especially for front-yard trees. If you're unsure, a quick email to the management company saves trouble.

## Common trees HOAs push back on removing

Healthy **live oaks** and other large shade trees are the hardest to get approved, because they define the look of the neighborhood. If a healthy tree is causing a problem, trimming or root management may be the approved alternative. If it's clearly causing damage, documentation is key. ## What if I already removed it?

Contact the HOA, explain why, share photos, and ask what's needed to resolve it. Offering to plant a replacement from their approved list usually helps.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need HOA approval to remove a tree in Leander?**
In most Leander HOAs, yes, at least for front-yard trees or trees over a certain size. Check your architectural guidelines or ask your management company.

**Will my HOA make me replace a tree I removed?**
Many do, especially for front-yard trees. They usually specify an approved species and minimum trunk size.

**Do I need approval to remove a dead tree?**
Rules vary, but dead trees are usually approved quickly. Photograph the tree before removal either way.

**How long does HOA approval take?**
Often a few days to a few weeks, depending on how often the committee meets. Emergency hazards can usually be handled faster.
