## 1. Nail pops and seam cracks: the first-years signature

Most drywall calls in the newer Georgetown builds are small and specific: nail pops and seam cracks in the first few years, as the house and the ground under it settle into each other over Georgetown limestone and clay. Inside, that shows up as the classic pattern: a diagonal crack over a doorway, a ceiling seam that reappears in the same joint, nail pops along one wall. In a newer home this is almost always cosmetic settling, not foundation failure - our [drywall cracks guide](/posts/drywall-cracks-when-to-worry-central-texas) walks through which cracks are which. The cracks that earn a specialist's look first are the ones that are wide, offset from one side to the other, growing fast, or paired with doors that suddenly stick.

The repair that lasts is cut back to solid material, re-tape, float, and match the existing texture before paint - a patch that does not match the surrounding texture is more obvious than the hole was, and newer smooth-finish interiors are the least forgiving of all. Details are on the [Georgetown drywall page](/services/drywall-repair-georgetown-tx).

## 2. The punch list: the year the builder warranty ends

In Georgetown Village it is the punch list that shows up the year the builder warranty ends: a door that will not latch, a bathroom fan that rattles, baseboard that came loose, three things that need mounting, caulk and grout lines that have opened up. Individually none of it is worth a service call; together it is a half day. The national builders carry the standard structure - one year on workmanship, two on systems, ten on structure - so the move while a window is still open is to build the list and file it with the builder first, while it is still their bill. Once the warranty is gone, the same list becomes a handyman list, priced by the job in half-day blocks, not by the hour. ## 3. Exterior paint and caulk: builder-grade, ready for a real color

Painting is mostly preparation, and in the newer Georgetown builds the starting point is builder-grade flat paint ready for a real color. The visible part - rolling color on a wall - is the fast part; what separates a paint job that looks good for a decade from one that peels in two years is what happens before the first coat: patching, caulking, sanding, priming and masking. Outside, the south and west elevations fail first in Central Texas sun, and caulk joints open at the trim and around windows - left alone, open joints are how water gets behind the elevation.

The order matters more than the brand: repair first, then caulk, prime and paint. Our [Georgetown painting page](/services/interior-exterior-painting-georgetown-tx) covers what a full repaint includes, and our [exterior paint guide](/posts/exterior-paint-texas-heat-how-long-lasts) covers how long a good job lasts in this heat. One Georgetown Village note: an exterior color change needs HOA approval first, even when the new color is close to the old one - get the approval in hand before the crew, not after. A repaint in the same color usually does not need it.

## 5. Builder trees, decks and drainage on limestone and clay

Georgetown's trees split by age, and Georgetown Village is on the young side of the split: fast-growing builder trees - red oaks, Arizona ash, hackberries - planted across the newer communities, now big enough to hang over roofs. The oaks among them run on Central Texas rules:

- **Timing.** Oak wilt spreads through fresh cuts during the high-risk months. The safe window runs July through January - which means right now, late fall and winter, is when oak work should happen. November through January is the heart of that window. Our [live oak trimming guide](/posts/live-oak-trimming-central-texas) covers raising, thinning and reducing, and what never to do to an oak.
- **Removal is the last resort, priced by size.** Removal in the Austin area averages about $736, with most jobs between $256 and $1,962 depending on height and access. Georgetown protects its heritage trees: a heritage tree, generally 24 inches or more in diameter, needs a city Heritage Tree Removal Permit before it can be removed or heavily pruned, and HOAs often require their own approval first - we flag which applies before quoting. The [Georgetown tree page](/services/tree-removal-georgetown-tx) has the full breakdown.

The ground itself is the other outdoor item. Across Georgetown the soil is thin over limestone, so footings have to be set right or a deck works loose as the clay swells and shrinks, and west-facing yards bake all afternoon while lots under mature oaks rot boards from the top down. New decks run $30-$50 per square foot, most projects $3,946-$10,909; repairs run $852-$3,345. ## What order to do it in

1. **Water first.** Caulk, open joints, drainage running toward the house - anything letting water in outranks everything cosmetic.
2. **Warranty-window items.** Anything that might be the builder's bill gets listed and filed before its window closes; the punch list that lands the year the warranty ends gets worked in one visit.
3. **Trees in the safe window.** Oak work between July and January, while the window is open - the builder trees do not get smaller for waiting.
4. **Surfaces.** Paint, then floors if the builder carpet is going, then washing as the finishing pass.

## Frequently asked questions

**What breaks first in a newer Georgetown Village home?**
The small stuff, in a predictable order: nail pops and seam cracks in the first few years as the house settles over limestone and clay, caulk joints opening at trim and windows, and builder carpet wearing out on the traffic lines. The punch list that shows up the year the builder warranty ends - latches, fans, baseboard, mounting - is the other half of the first cycle. The structure itself, on a maintained home, is rarely the problem.

**How much does it cost to repaint a Georgetown Village house?**
Interior rooms run $350 to $650 for a bedroom, walls only, and a whole 2,000 sq ft interior runs $4,000 to $8,000 for walls or $6,500 to $12,000 with ceilings and trim. Exterior pricing depends on size, stories, elevation materials and how much caulk and crack repair the prep uncovers, so it is quoted as a written estimate after a walk-through rather than a per-foot guess.

**Are the drywall cracks in my Georgetown Village home a foundation problem?**
Usually not. Lots over limestone and clay move slightly with every wet and dry cycle, and in newer construction the nail pops, seam cracks and diagonal cracks over doorways are the normal signature of that movement in the first few years. Cracks that are wide, growing fast, offset from one side to the other, or paired with doors suddenly sticking deserve a foundation specialist's look before any cosmetic repair - and if the home is still inside its structural warranty window, file it with the builder first.

**When should I trim my Georgetown Village oaks?**
Between July and January. Oak wilt spreads through fresh pruning cuts most easily in the spring high-risk period, so late fall and winter are the safe working window in Central Texas, and November through January is the heart of it. Trimming runs about $245 to $1,029 depending on size and canopy, and cuts should be painted immediately as part of the job. The builder trees planted across the community are now big enough to hang over roofs, so the scope is real canopy work, not a street-tree trim.

**Does Georgetown Village have an HOA I need approval from for repairs?**
For like-for-like repairs, generally no approval is needed. An exterior color change needs HOA approval before work starts, even when the new color is close to the old one, and exterior changes, additions and anything that alters the footprint or elevation usually need an HOA architectural submittal first. Removing a large tree can need a city heritage-tree permit and HOA approval as well. We flag which applies during the walk-through and get any needed approval in hand before work starts.
