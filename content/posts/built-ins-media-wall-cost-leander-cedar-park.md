# What Built-Ins and Media Walls Actually Cost in Leander & Cedar Park (2026 Numbers)

Search "built-ins cost" and you get a per-linear-foot number. That number is a starting point, not a price, and the gap between the low end and the high end of it is the whole story.

A linear foot of open painted shelving is a box and some shelves. A linear foot of cabinetry with doors, drawers, crown and concealed lighting is furniture built into your wall. Both get quoted "per foot," and one costs three times the other — which is why two quotes for the same wall can land thousands of dollars apart and both be honest numbers for different work.

Here is what built-ins, closet build-outs and media walls actually cost in Leander, Cedar Park, Georgetown and greater Austin, and what moves the number.

## Why the range is so wide

### Doors and drawers are the multiplier

Open shelving is the cheapest built-in there is: boxes, shelves, face frame, paint. Every door adds hinges, alignment and finishing time. Every drawer adds a box, slides and a front that has to sit perfectly in line with its neighbours. A wall of open shelves and a wall of closed cabinetry can be the same size and differ by $3,000 or more, and the closed version is usually the right call around a TV — it is where the remotes, consoles and cable clutter actually go.

### Paint grade vs. stain grade

Painted built-ins are built from paint-grade material, and paint hides a multitude of small sins — joints get filled, sanded and disappear. Stained hardwood shows the grain, which means it shows everything: the wood costs more, the joinery has to be cleaner, and the finishing takes longer. Stain typically prices noticeably above the same design painted, and it is worth it in exactly the rooms where the wood is the point — a library wall, a fireplace surround. Everywhere else, paint is the smarter money.

### The wall itself

Walls in production homes are rarely square and floors are rarely level, and a built-in has nowhere to hide that — it is scribed to the wall and sits tight to the floor, so the out-of-square gets absorbed in the build, not in a gap you stare at. Newer homes in Twin Creeks and Ranch at Brushy Creek are usually the easy case. Older homes in Anderson Mill and Cypress Creek, and the mudroom and garage-adjacent walls in Buttercup Creek, take more fitting time. That fitting is a real line in the price even though you will never see it itemised.

### Height

A built-in that stops at eight feet and one that runs floor to ceiling are different amounts of material, ladder work and trim. Crown that ties into the ceiling is the detail that makes a built-in read as part of the house instead of furniture pushed against a wall — and it is priced like the extra work it is.

### Lighting and power

Concealed shelf lighting, a moved outlet for the TV, or a circuit for an electric fireplace insert turns a carpentry job into a carpentry-plus-electrical job. The insert itself is also a separate cost that varies widely by size and brand, on top of the wall built around it. This is the part of a media wall quote most likely to be vague, so ask for it as its own line: what the wall costs, what the insert costs, and what the electrical costs. Our part is the wall and the channels; the electrical work itself is run by our licensed partners.

## The media wall, specifically

A media wall is not a big bookshelf. It is a framed bump-out built over your existing wall: framing, a recess or mount for the TV, every cable routed inside the wall, shelving or cabinetry on one or both sides, and the whole thing trimmed and painted so the room looks built around it.

That structure is why the planning ranges start around $5,000 for a straightforward TV feature wall and run to $10,000 and up once an electric fireplace, closed storage and lighting are in the design. If a quote for a full media wall comes in far under that, look at what is missing — usually the electrical, the insert, or the finish paint, all of which you will pay for later anyway.

One decision to make early: TV size. The recess and the cabinet layout are built around the screen. Changing from a 65-inch to an 85-inch TV after the wall is framed is a rebuild, not an adjustment.

## What you can reasonably do yourself

Honest assessment, including the parts that argue against hiring anyone:

**Worth doing yourself:**
- Painting a simple, already-built shelving unit. Flat paint on boxes is genuinely forgiving.
- A closet organiser kit in a reach-in closet. The flat-pack systems are designed for it and a Saturday finishes it.
- Measuring and photographing the wall, and deciding what actually has to live in the piece — books, storage, media gear, or display. That decision drives the design more than any material choice.

**Not worth doing yourself:**
- Scribing a tall unit to an out-of-square wall. This is the skill the whole job turns on, and a gap at the ceiling line is visible from across the room forever.
- The electrical in a media wall. Outlets and circuits inside a closed wall are licensed work in Texas, full stop.
- Stain-grade finishing on hardwood. Paint forgives; stain documents.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much do built-ins cost in Cedar Park TX?**
For a standard painted bookshelf project, about $1,000 to $2,000 in Angi's Austin-area data. Built-in cabinets with doors and drawers run $2,000 to $7,500 for most projects — around $4,500 on average, or $200 to $600 per linear foot. A custom closet build-out in Austin averages about $2,670. Size, doors, drawers and stain-grade hardwood are what move a project up the range.

**How much does a media wall cost in Leander TX?**
Plan on $5,000 to $7,000 for a TV feature wall with a decorative finish, $7,000 to $10,000 with an electric fireplace, shelving and lighting, and $10,000 to $15,000 or more for a complete custom composition. The fireplace insert and the electrical work are separate cost drivers inside those totals, and a good quote breaks them out as their own lines.

**Are built-ins priced per linear foot?**
Often, yes — $200 to $600 per linear foot for built-in cabinets is the common planning range. But the per-foot number only means something once you know what a foot includes: open shelves price at the bottom, doors and drawers in the middle, and stain-grade hardwood with lighting at the top. Compare quotes on what is in the foot, not just the foot.

**Does a media wall need electrical work?**
Almost always some. The TV outlet usually moves, cables get routed inside the wall, and an electric fireplace insert needs its own power. In Texas that work is done by a licensed electrician — on our jobs the electrical is handled by our licensed partners and managed by Sanches Group as part of the one project.

**Painted or stained — which costs more?**
Stained, every time. The hardwood costs more than paint-grade material, the joinery has to be cleaner because nothing is hidden, and the finishing takes longer. Paint is the right choice for most rooms; stain earns its price on a library wall or a fireplace surround where the wood is the feature.

**How long does a built-in or media wall take?**
Most built-ins are a few days on site once the material is in. A media wall with framing, electrical and a fireplace insert runs longer — the electrical and the finish work set the pace, not the boxes. Anything with a lot of doors, drawers or stain work takes longer again. The schedule belongs in the written estimate.

**Do built-ins need a permit in Leander or Cedar Park?**
Carpentry on its own generally does not. New electrical circuits do, and that permit is part of the electrical partner's work on a media wall. If a project touches structure — removing a load-bearing wall to open a room up, for example — that is a different conversation and should be permitted and engineered as one.
